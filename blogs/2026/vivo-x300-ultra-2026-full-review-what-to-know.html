Vivo X300 Ultra 2026: Full Review & What to Know

June 29, 2026 8-Minute Read

Most premium Android flagships in 2026 compete on specs sheets that look nearly identical at a glance. The Vivo X300 Ultra doesn’t bother with that race. It builds a different argument entirely — one centred on imaging hardware so specialized that it has more in common with a mirrorless camera body than a phone.

This is Vivo’s first “Ultra” device to launch globally, not just in China. That alone makes it historically significant. But the real story is whether the hardware justifies the price — and that answer is more complicated than the marketing suggests.

The Camera Is Not One Camera — It’s Four Distinct Optical Instruments

The rear system pairs a 200MP Sony LYT-901 main sensor with a 200MP Samsung ISOCELL HP0 periscope telephoto. A 50MP ultrawide and a dedicated 50MP True Color lens with 12 spectral channels round out the quad setup. [Android Headlines]

That spectral sensor matters more than it sounds. Standard phone cameras capture three channels of colour data — red, green, blue. The 12-channel True Color lens captures colour and ambient lighting with near-perfect accuracy, specifically to eliminate the processed, artificial look endemic to smartphone photography. [Android Headlines] It’s measuring light the way a spectrometer does, not the way a normal camera does.

The main camera shoots at a 35mm equivalent focal length — a deliberate, documentary-style choice favoured by street photographers for its naturalistic perspective. [Kimovil] Most phone makers default to 24mm or 26mm because wider looks dramatic. Vivo chose restraint. That matters for anyone shooting candid or travel work.

The telephoto is where things get genuinely unprecedented. The periscope camera achieves CIPA 7.0-rated gimbal-level OIS — a stabilization standard previously limited to dedicated camera equipment — delivering 1.5 degrees of correction across all axes. [Homescreen] What that means in practice: handheld shots at 85mm that would blur on virtually any other phone hold sharp.

Two External Lens Attachments That Actually Change What You Can Shoot

Vivo offers a “Cannon 400” teleconverter delivering a native 17.4x optical zoom — equivalent to a 400mm lens — and a more compact “Lipstick 200” providing an 8.7x boost. Both leverage Zeiss optics, support gimbal-grade OIS, and track subjects at up to 60fps autofocus. [Android Headlines]

This is modular in a meaningful way. The 400mm extender is viable for wildlife and sports photography. The 200mm alternative, weighing 153g, is light enough to leave attached all day. Neither requires proprietary cables or software bridges — they mount optically via a case-based lens mount.

A camera grip, modelled in collaboration with SmallRig, pairs with both extenders to replicate the handling feel of a traditional camera, including physical shutter buttons and cooling fans built into the grip itself. [Tech Advisor]

The cooling fan in the grip is not cosmetic. Under sustained 4K video recording at high frame rates, heat builds in the phone body — something we’ll come back to.

Professional Video That Belongs on a Proper Set

The X300 Ultra debuts support for the APV codec — a professional-tier format designed for higher quality and easier post-production workflows. Users can record 4K at 120fps in Log format, apply custom 3D LUTs directly inside the viewfinder in real time, and use a quad-microphone setup for immersive audio. [Android Headlines]

The LOG compatibility with ACES colour science is not a footnote. ACES is the colour standard used by major film studios. Vivo says X300 Ultra footage integrates directly into professional cinema camera workflows without requiring colour space conversion [Gogi Tech] — meaning a filmmaker can cut phone-captured B-roll against RED or ARRI primary footage without a grading mismatch.

That’s a claim. Verify it in your own workflow. But the architecture is there.

The Chip, the Battery, and the Thermal Problem Nobody Advertises

The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 — built on a 3nm process — scores approximately 4,010,000 on AnTuTu, paired with up to 16GB of LPDDR5X RAM and UFS 4.1 storage up to 1TB. [Kimovil] On paper, this is the fastest Android hardware available in 2026.

But speed has a cost. Heat.

Notebookcheck’s review notes pronounced thermal throttling and high heat emissions under sustained load — a significant finding that positions the X300 Ultra’s cooling system as a meaningful engineering weakness. [Notebookcheck] The chip is capable. The thermal management doesn’t always keep pace with it. That matters most if you’re shooting 4K120 video for extended runs, which is exactly the use case Vivo is pitching this phone for.

The battery is a silicon-carbon cell with 6,600mAh capacity — one of the highest-density cells in a flagship-class body that measures just 8.19mm thick. 100W wired charging refills it in under 40 minutes. Wireless charging runs at 40W. [Homescreen] Silicon-carbon anodes store more lithium ions than traditional graphite, which is the reason Vivo can fit this capacity into that chassis without making the phone feel like a brick.

European variants retain the full 6,600mAh capacity — confirmed by Vivo to Android Authority — rather than the reduced batteries some manufacturers use for Western markets. [Android Headlines]

The Display Is WQHD+ at 144Hz. It’s Also Not the Best in Class.

The 6.82-inch LTPO OLED panel runs at 1440x3168 resolution with a 144Hz adaptive refresh rate, peaking at 1,800 nits of brightness. [Gogi Tech]

It is a very good display. It is not the sharpest display on the market in 2026. That’s not a deal-breaker — 1440p at 6.82 inches is perceptually sharp to the human eye at any normal viewing distance. But if the panel is what you’re buying, the Galaxy S26 Ultra competes directly.

Software That Doesn’t Get Talked About Enough

The phone runs OriginOS 6, based on Android 16. Vivo has committed to five years of Android OS upgrades and seven years of security patches. [Gogi Tech]

Seven years of security support means this phone is viable until 2033. That’s the same commitment Apple and Samsung have started making, and it fundamentally changes the value calculation. A $1,300 phone that works safely and receives feature updates for seven years is a different financial proposition than one that ages out in three.

Where It Loses to the Competition — Honestly

Notebookcheck’s assessment finds the X300 Ultra to be, outside of the camera system, occasionally a shade behind both the OPPO Find X9 Ultra and the Galaxy S26 Ultra in overall execution. [Notebookcheck]

The OPPO Find X9 Ultra wins on native 10x optical zoom without any accessories, a brighter f/1.5 aperture lens, and faster wireless charging. The X300 Ultra answers with the ZEISS ecosystem, a thinner body, a dramatically larger telephoto sensor (200MP vs. 50MP at 10x), and the professional video accessory system. [Homescreen]

The trade-off is real. If you want the best all-round phone, the competition is legitimately competitive. If you want the most capable photographic instrument in a smartphone form factor — particularly for telephoto and video work — the X300 Ultra has no direct equivalent.

Pricing: China vs. Global — A Gap Worth Understanding

In China, the X300 Ultra starts at 6,999 yuan (approximately $1,013) for the 12GB/256GB model, rising to 8,999 yuan (approximately $1,302) for the 16GB/1TB configuration. [Android Headlines]

The global pricing is steeper. The European RRP for the 16GB/1TB model sits at €1,999 [Notebookcheck] — roughly $2,200 at current rates. That’s a significant premium over the Chinese launch price, driven partly by import logistics, warranty infrastructure, and the economic pressures Vivo flagged ahead of global expansion.

This marks the first time a Vivo Ultra device has launched outside China — a structural shift for the brand, not just a product decision. [Tech Advisor] Vivo historically kept its top hardware domestic. The fact that it didn’t this cycle signals confidence in both the product and in its ability to compete in mature Western markets.

Who This Is Actually For

The X300 Ultra is not a phone that tries to be everything. It is a phone built around a specific, demanding use case: serious photography and video work, from people who would otherwise carry a separate camera.

The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 handles everything else — gaming, multitasking, AI tasks — at the highest level available in 2026. The thermal throttling is a real issue for sustained workloads. The colour accuracy problems that reviewers flagged at launch were partially addressed in a May 2026 software update, which improved colour reproduction and power management, [Notebookcheck] though some inconsistency remains.

It has two genuinely excellent 200MP cameras and arguably the best ultrawide camera hardware shipping in any 2026 flagship. [Android Headlines]

That’s a meaningful sentence. Not “one of the best.” The best.

If that’s what you’re buying for, the X300 Ultra delivers. If you want a more rounded, thermally stable flagship at a lower price, the OPPO Find X9 Ultra and Galaxy S26 Ultra are serious alternatives that won’t embarrass anyone.

The X300 Ultra isn’t for everyone. It was never designed to be.
