Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5: Everything You Need to Know

June 10, 2026 9-Minute Read

Most AI model launches feel incremental. A few benchmark points here, a speed improvement there. Andrej Karpathy called Claude Fable 5 “a major-version-bump-deserving step change forward.” [TrueFoundry] That is not the kind of quote someone throws around casually.

On June 9, 2026, Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5: a Mythos-class model made safe for general use. [Anthropic] If you want the short version: it is the most capable AI model Anthropic has ever released to the public, it shares the same underlying architecture as the previously restricted Claude Mythos system, and it costs $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens.

Here is everything else you need to know.

Fable 5 and Mythos 5 Are the Same Model — With One Critical Difference

Claude Fable 5 uses the same underlying model as Claude Mythos 5 but ships with guardrails that block responses in high-risk areas like cybersecurity, biology, chemistry, and distillation. In those cases, it falls back to Claude Opus 4.8 to deliver a safe answer. [LushBinary]

That distinction is not cosmetic. It reflects a genuine decision Anthropic made under pressure: the Mythos tier of models is capable enough in adversarial cybersecurity contexts that simply releasing it without restriction was considered untenable. Anthropic stress-tested its classifiers with jailbreak attempts before releasing Fable 5. Internally, they ran an external bug bounty that produced no universal jailbreaks in over 1,000 hours of testing, and external red-teaming organizations also failed to find universal jailbreaks. [TechCrunch]

This is the mechanism behind the public launch becoming possible at all: not a watered-down model, but a full-capability model with a safety layer sitting on top.

The safeguards are tuned conservatively — they’ll sometimes catch harmless requests, though they trigger, on average, in less than 5% of sessions. [Anthropic] For most professional use cases, you will rarely notice them.

Mythos 5 Remains Restricted, and the Reasons Are Serious

Claude Mythos 5 is the same underlying model as Fable 5, but with the safeguards lifted in some areas. It will initially be deployed through Project Glasswing, in collaboration with the US government, as an upgrade to Claude Mythos Preview. [anthropic]

The announcement follows Anthropic’s April 2025 rollout of Claude Mythos Preview through Project Glasswing, a restricted program for cyber defenders, critical infrastructure providers, and major software maintainers. Anthropic created Glasswing after internal evaluations showed Mythos-class models could find and exploit software vulnerabilities at a level that raised meaningful misuse concerns. [VentureBeat]

This is not standard corporate caution. When a company voluntarily delays its most commercially valuable product because of what it can do to software infrastructure, that tells you something about what the model actually is.

Last week, Anthropic expanded Mythos access to hundreds of organizations across 15 countries, again focusing on organizations that manage critical infrastructure. [TechCrunch] Mythos 5 follows that same path — broader than before, but still gated.

The Benchmark Numbers Are Not Ordinary

Fable 5 posts 80.3% on SWE-Bench Pro while the next-best model sits 11 points behind. [TrueFoundry] SWE-Bench Pro tests real software engineering tasks from production codebases. An 11-point gap at that difficulty level is large.

The real-world numbers are more striking than the benchmarks.

During early testing, Stripe reported that Fable 5 compressed months of engineering into days. In a 50-million-line Ruby codebase, the model performed a codebase-wide migration in a day that would otherwise have taken a whole team over two months by hand. [anthropic] That is not a benchmark. That is a shipping team’s actual codebase. That result happened.

On Cognition’s FrontierCode evaluation — which tests whether models can pass difficult coding tasks while meeting the standards of high-quality production codebases — Fable 5 scores highest among frontier models, even at medium effort. [anthropic] “Medium effort” matters here because it signals token efficiency. Expensive models that require maximum effort to be useful are expensive twice.

Knowledge Work and Finance: Senior-Level Performance

On Hebbia’s Finance Benchmark for senior-level reasoning, Fable 5 has the highest score of any model, with substantial gains in document-based reasoning, chart and table interpretation, and problem solving. IMC noted that Fable 5 aced their trading-analysis evaluations nearly across the board, including factual lookup, conceptual reasoning, root-cause analysis, and expected-value analysis. [anthropic]

The reason this matters structurally: financial analysis tasks tend to punish hallucination immediately. A model that makes things up gets caught fast when the numbers don’t reconcile. Acing those evaluations signals something more meaningful than general benchmark performance.

Vision and Memory: Two Capabilities That Change Agent Design

Fable 5 is the new state-of-the-art model for tasks involving vision. It can extract precise numbers from detailed scientific figures and can perform complex vision-based tasks like rebuilding a web app’s source code from screenshots alone. Previous Claude models struggled to play Pokémon FireRed even with harnesses that gave them additional helpful tools, but Fable 5 beat FireRed with a minimal, vision-only harness. [anthropic]

Less scaffolding required means lower infrastructure overhead. That compounds in production.

On memory and long-context, Fable 5 stays focused across millions of tokens in long-running tasks and improves its outputs using its own notes. When tested playing the deck-building game Slay the Spire, giving Fable 5 access to persistent file-based memory improved its performance three times more than for Opus 4.8. Fable also reached the game’s final act three times more often. [anthropic]

The mechanism here matters: it is not just that Fable 5 has a larger context window. It is that the model makes better use of memory when memory is available. That ratio difference is what changes what kinds of agentic tasks become viable.

Mythos 5’s Science Results Are the Part Most People Are Underreacting To

Using Mythos 5, Anthropic’s internal protein design experts accelerated aspects of the drug design process by around ten times. Mythos 5, with protein design and bioinformatics tools but no human assistance, matches or beats skilled human operators — executing all of the tasks that are normally completed by a scientist: choosing binding sites, selecting and running protein design tools, and recovering from failures along the way. Nine of the 14 protein targets from this study yielded strong candidates for drug design currently under investigation. [anthropic]

That is not a demo. That is a controlled internal study.

Mythos 5 is Anthropic’s first model to consistently produce novel, compelling scientific hypotheses. In blinded head-to-head comparisons against Opus-class models, scientists preferred Mythos’s molecular biology hypotheses approximately 80% of the time, and have advanced several to experimental evaluation. One Mythos hypothesis — a novel mechanism for an E. coli protein — was corroborated in a study from a lab independently working on the same problem. [anthropic]

Independent corroboration. That is the standard science uses. It cleared it.

In genomics, Mythos 5 conducted novel research in over a week of largely autonomous work. It assembled single-cell data for millions of cells spanning 138 animal species, and its trained model outperformed a recent model published in the journal Science — despite being 100 times smaller. [anthropic]

Pricing: What It Actually Costs and When the Free Window Closes

Fable 5 and Mythos 5 are priced at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens. [Anthropic] That is double the price of Opus 4.8, which runs $5 input and $25 output.

On the Claude API and consumption-based Enterprise plans, Fable 5 is fully available from today. For subscription plans, Fable 5 is included on Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans at no extra cost from launch through June 22. On June 23, Anthropic will remove Fable 5 from those plans. Using it after that will require usage credits. If capacity allows, they’ll extend the included window. [Anthropic]

On Claude.ai subscription plans, Fable 5 counts as 2x usage. [TrueFoundry] Budget accordingly.

For developers, the API model string is claude-fable-5. The context window is 1M tokens, with up to 128k output tokens. [Coding Liquids]

Fable 5 is also available on Amazon Bedrock in the US East (N. Virginia) and Europe (Stockholm) regions, and on the Claude Platform on AWS across North America, South America, Europe, and Asia Pacific. [AWS] For teams using GitHub Copilot, Fable 5 is rolling out to Copilot Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise users, including VS Code, Visual Studio, JetBrains, Xcode, Eclipse, Copilot CLI, GitHub Copilot cloud agent, github.com, and mobile surfaces. [Coursiv Blog]

One Operational Detail Most Teams Will Miss

Fable 5 now requires 30-day data retention. [LushBinary] If your workflows run under zero-retention agreements, that requirement changes your options. Verify your data policy before routing production traffic through it.

The Honest Picture

Fable 5 is real progress. Not “real progress” in the marketing sense. In the sense that the gap between what it can do and what its predecessor could do is wide enough to change what kinds of tasks are worth automating. The Stripe codebase migration alone — two engineer-months compressed to a day — is the kind of number that reorganizes how engineering teams think about sprint planning.

Mythos 5’s science results are further-reaching and will take longer for most people to internalize. A model that produces independently corroborated molecular biology hypotheses and outperforms published Science journal models in genomics is operating in territory that most AI benchmarks were never designed to measure.

Anthropic says Fable 5’s broad release is possible because of new safeguards that block responses in specific high-risk areas. It’s also capitalizing on growing momentum and investor interest in its technology ahead of a potentially massive IPO expected to take place as soon as this year. [CNBC] That context is real. But the capabilities are also real. Both things are true at once. Judge what you can verify.
