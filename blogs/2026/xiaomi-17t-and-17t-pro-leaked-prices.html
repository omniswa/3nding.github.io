Xiaomi 17T and 17T Pro: Leaked Prices

May 25, 2026 7-Minute Read

The Xiaomi T-series has spent years earning its reputation as a flagship experience at a price that doesn’t require an existential justification. That positioning may be shifting. With the Xiaomi 17T and 17T Pro set to launch globally on May 28, 2026, a wave of regional pricing leaks — from Russia, the UAE, the Philippines, and Europe — paints a picture of two phones pushing closer to the premium tier than the brand’s traditional sweet spot. Global prices are estimated to range between $700 and $1,150 depending on the model and configuration. [LatestLY] That’s not “flagship killer” territory anymore. It’s just... flagship.

What the Leaks Say About Pricing

The most concrete European figures come from Dealabs, a reliable source for pre-launch retail data. The Xiaomi 17T is tipped to start at €749 in France for the 12GB/256GB variant, while the Xiaomi 17T Pro could be priced at €999 for the 12GB/512GB model. [GSMArena]

Those numbers hold up reasonably well when you cross-reference other regions. In the Philippines, the 17T is expected to start at PHP 33,999 (roughly $553) for the 12GB/256GB version, with the 512GB variant at approximately $618. The Pro starts at PHP 45,999 ($748) for 256GB and goes to PHP 47,999 ($780) for 512GB. [Gizmochina]

In the UAE, the standard 17T is tipped at AED 2,599, converting to around $708. Russia’s listings put the 17T Pro at approximately $963 for 256GB, with a 1TB variant surfacing at around $1,150. [ProPakistani]

A quick note on interpreting these numbers: direct currency conversions rarely land at final retail prices. Taxes, import duties, and Xiaomi’s own regional pricing strategy all introduce variables. The Philippine price is lower than Europe’s partly because of local purchasing power considerations and market competition. Europe is historically where Xiaomi prices its global lineup most aggressively in the premium direction.

What You’re Actually Getting for the Money

Pricing only makes sense in context of specs. The 17T is expected to run MediaTek’s Dimensity 8500 Ultra chipset paired with 12GB LPDDR5X RAM and up to 512GB UFS 4.1 storage. The display is a 6.59-inch AMOLED panel with 120Hz refresh rate and 1.5K resolution. [PhoneBunch]

The Pro model steps up to the Dimensity 9500 and a 7,000mAh battery. Charging also gets a significant upgrade, with 100W wired and 50W wireless support versus the standard model’s 67W wired-only setup. [Gizmochina]

On paper, the Dimensity 8500 Ultra is a capable chip — more than fast enough for daily use, gaming included. The Dimensity 9500, however, sits in a different category. It uses an all-big-core CPU architecture and delivers performance closer to what you’d expect from a Snapdragon 8 Elite device. That gap matters if you’re deciding between the two models.

Camera hardware is where the two phones diverge in a subtler way. Both feature Leica-branded triple rear setups with a 50MP telephoto at 5x optical zoom and a 12MP ultrawide, but the Pro uses a newer Light Fusion 950 primary sensor compared to the Light Fusion 800 on the standard 17T. [Gizmochina] In practice, the Light Fusion 950 should perform better in low-light conditions and high-contrast scenes. It’s not a dramatic jump, but it’s meaningful at the price gap between the two.

Build quality also differs: the standard 17T features a plastic frame, while the 17T Pro uses a more premium metal construction. [GSMArena] At €749 for a plastic-framed phone, that’s a detail worth sitting with.

The Calendar Shift No One Is Talking About Enough

The T-series has typically arrived around September over the years. The 17T and 17T Pro are now expected in May — roughly four months ahead of the usual schedule. [91Mobiles]

That’s not a minor adjustment. Xiaomi is essentially compressing its product calendar, which creates a few downstream effects. Buyers who purchased the 15T Pro last autumn are looking at a successor less than eight months later. And for anyone who held off specifically waiting for the T-series, that wait just got shorter — which is good, but the compressed timeline also means less time between generations for meaningful hardware differentiation.

The move likely reflects MediaTek’s chip availability and Xiaomi’s desire to capitalize on the Dimensity 9500’s launch momentum before competitors running the same silicon get established. It may also reflect pressure from Samsung’s Galaxy S25 series and the broader premium Android market, where timing matters more than it once did.

The India Situation Is Telling

The 17T Pro is not expected to launch in India, with the 17T positioned in the ₹74,999–₹84,999 range to compete with high-end devices locally. [MSN]

That’s a deliberate product segmentation call. India’s smartphone market is intensely competitive in the ₹50,000–₹80,000 range, with Samsung, OnePlus, and POCO all fighting for the same buyer. Bringing in the Pro at ₹1,10,000+ would mean competing against Apple and Samsung’s Galaxy S-series in a market where those brands carry significant status weight. Xiaomi is apparently choosing not to fight that battle in India, at least not with the T-series.

This also tells you something about who the 17T Pro is actually aimed at: the European and Middle Eastern consumer who wants flagship performance, accepts Xiaomi’s brand positioning, and is willing to pay close to €1,000 for it. That’s a narrower audience than Xiaomi’s traditional base, and it’s a bet that the brand has enough equity in those markets to sustain it.

The Counterpoint Worth Considering

Here’s the thing about a €999 Xiaomi: you’re competing directly against the Samsung Galaxy S25 and iPhone 16. Both of those phones carry extensive software support timelines, proven resale values, and brand ecosystems that Xiaomi simply can’t match at this point.

If accurate, Xiaomi seems to be positioning the 17T lineup much closer to premium flagships this year rather than the traditional “flagship killer” category the T-series was once known for. [Gizmochina] That repositioning isn’t automatically a mistake, but it does demand that the hardware, software longevity, and brand perception all hold up at that price. If HyperOS update commitments remain vague and resale value tanks after 18 months, buyers at €999 will feel that in a way they wouldn’t at €649.

The standard 17T, by contrast, looks more defensible on value grounds — especially in markets like the Philippines where it undercuts European pricing meaningfully. A €749 phone with a Dimensity 8500 Ultra, Leica cameras, and a 6,500mAh battery is asking a lot, but asking it in a range where Android buyers have fewer truly premium options.

What to Watch at the May 28 Launch

A few things will determine whether these leaked prices are actually justified:

The software commitment matters most. Xiaomi announcing a concrete multi-year update schedule for HyperOS would do more for the 17T Pro’s €999 case than any spec improvement. Without it, buyers at that price are taking a real risk.

Real-world charging speed also needs scrutiny. 100W wired sounds impressive, but thermal management and the actual time-to-full in practice often diverge from headline numbers.

And then there’s the India pricing — the final confirmed number for the 17T will signal a lot about how aggressively Xiaomi intends to price the T-series globally across mid-premium markets going forward.

The leaks have given us most of the picture. The May 28 event will fill in the edges.
