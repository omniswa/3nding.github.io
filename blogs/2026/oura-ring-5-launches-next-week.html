Oura Ring 5 Launches Next Week

May 22, 2026 7-Minute Read

Oura didn’t announce anything. No countdown timer, no teaser campaign, no carefully choreographed “one more thing” moment. Instead, someone posted a photo of an internal document on Reddit, and now every tech outlet is running the same story. The Oura Ring 5 is launching next week — May 28 for the announcement and pre-orders, June 4 for actual deliveries. That’s the claim. And unlike a lot of leaks, this one is harder to dismiss than usual.

What the Leaked Document Actually Says

The document — reportedly internal launch material — points to a May 28 announcement with pre-orders opening that same day, followed by an official launch on June 4. The photo of the ring and its charging case in the document matches render leaks from March that Oura moved quickly to suppress — the company reportedly asked Android Headlines to remove those images shortly after publication.

The description within the document suggests the Oura Ring 5 is smaller and more comfortable than its predecessor, and offers the “same” health and subscription features as the Oura Ring 4. That last word — same — is the one worth sitting with.

It means no sweeping new biometrics at launch. No blood pressure readings, no FDA-cleared features Oura has been quietly working toward. What it suggests instead is a hardware refinement cycle: tighten the form factor, improve the sensors’ efficiency, and ship a ring that feels better on your finger than the Ring 4 did.

That might sound underwhelming. For most people who’ve worn the Ring 4 daily for a year or two, it’s probably the right call.

The Evidence Trail Leading Here

This leak didn’t arrive out of nowhere. The case for a 2026 Ring 5 has been building since early spring.

An FCC filing emerged in April under ID 2AD7V-OURA2602, listing the ring as model OA13 and a refreshed charger as OA14. The filing includes a 180-day confidentiality window, pushing hidden materials — like internal photos and the user manual — to early September 2026.

Then came the slip. A sizing video on Oura’s own website referred to “Oura Ring 5,” adding weight to a 2026 launch. The kind of mistake that only happens when a product is close enough to finished that internal materials have already been prepared.

The screenshot shared on Reddit describes the Ring 5 as a “smaller” and “more comfortable” ring while saying it keeps the same health-tracking capabilities and membership features as Oura Ring 4, suggesting Oura may be focusing more on refinement than a major feature overhaul. The screenshot lists late May for the announcement, pre-orders and sizing kits, with launch and shipments expected a week later.

That timing lines up cleanly with the FCC paper trail and the accidental website mention. Three independent signals pointing at the same window is not a coincidence.

What’s Actually Changing

Design is the story here. Images that first surfaced in March 2026 show the Ring 5 with a slightly more curved outer surface than the Ring 4. The shape change should make the ring read more like jewelry on the hand, not just a sleep tracker with a metal shell. The transition to the synthetic resin inner band is more seamless — the sort of industrial-design change users feel every time they put the ring on.

The color lineup is getting refreshed too. Rose Gold is out, and Deep Rose is in. Other finishes shown in the leaks include Gold, Silver, and Matte Black — six options total, which is a meaningful expansion.

On the sensor side, there’s one notable hardware upgrade buried in the earlier leaks. Notebookcheck reported that the Ring 5 optical sensors are significantly larger than those in the Ring 4, with additional red and green LEDs expected to improve heart rate and SpO2 accuracy. That’s not a flashy headline feature, but larger sensors with better LED arrays is how you get more accurate readings without changing what the ring claims to measure. It’s the kind of change that shows up in the data over time rather than in a spec sheet bullet point.

The charging case will be able to top up the ring four times, according to the leaked document. Oura introduced a portable case for the Ring 4 as a 2026 accessory — it looks like the Ring 5 ships with something similar built into the package.

The Pricing Question Nobody Can Answer Yet

No word on pricing was included in the leaked document, though it would not be surprising if it climbs another $50, especially with the RingConn Gen 3 nipping at its heels.

The Ring 4 launched at $349 with a $5.99/month membership required to access most of the data it collects. Over three years, that’s $216 on top of the $350 hardware cost — a total ownership price of $566. If the Ring 5 opens at $399, the three-year math gets worse, and Oura’s competitive window narrows further.

This is worth naming directly: the subscription is a structural disadvantage in 2026. It’s not that $6/month is ruinous. It’s that two of Oura’s most credible competitors — RingConn and Ultrahuman — have built their entire positioning around the fact that you don’t pay one.

The Timing Is Not Accidental

Here’s the non-obvious part: the Ring 5 is announcing the same week the RingConn Gen 3 ships.

RingConn Gen 3 preorders opened May 5th, 2026, with an official launch date of May 29th. Oura’s announcement is May 28. That’s not a coincidence — it’s a counter-programming move. Announce the Ring 5 the day before the RingConn Gen 3 actually lands in customers’ hands, and you absorb some of the media oxygen that would otherwise go to the competitor.

Whether it works depends on what Oura announces. If pricing is aggressive and the design holds up in person the way it does in renders, the news cycle runs their direction. If pricing is high and the sensor story is thin, reviewers will spend the next two weeks comparing it to a ring that costs less and doesn’t charge a monthly fee.

Based on current leaks for the Oura Ring 5, there’s no indication that battery life is getting a major upgrade — a notable gap given that the Ring 4’s real-world five-to-six-day battery is already a weak point against competitors rated at ten-plus days.

The Blood Pressure Storyline Running in the Background

Longer term, Oura is working toward something bigger. Oura has filed patents around blood pressure monitoring from the finger and is actively pursuing FDA clearance for that feature. Those moves don’t affect what ships next week, but they explain why Oura isn’t rushing to add experimental features to the Ring 5. Getting FDA clearance for a blood pressure reading from a ring is a multi-year regulatory process. You don’t want to be revising your sensor stack mid-clearance.

If that clearance comes through — and the patent activity suggests Oura believes it eventually will — the Ring 5 hardware may be the platform it runs on. That’s speculative, but it reframes the “same sensors” story slightly. This might not be a generation that lacks ambition. It might be one that’s laying groundwork.

Should You Wait or Buy Now?

If you’re currently on the Ring 3 and have been waiting for a meaningful upgrade, the Ring 5 is probably worth the wait — assuming the May 28 announcement confirms what the leaks suggest. Smaller, better-fitting, more accurate sensors, and six color options is a solid generational step.

If you’re on the Ring 4 and it’s working well, there’s no compelling reason to upgrade based on what’s leaked so far. No new health metrics, no battery life jump, no features that aren’t already in your app. Wait for real reviews.

If you’re considering the smart ring category for the first time and subscription fees bother you, the RingConn Gen 3 launching this same week is a genuine alternative worth evaluating before defaulting to the Oura name-brand premium.

One week for the announcement. Two for first hands-on reviews. That’s usually enough time to know whether the leaks held up.

Updated May 22, 2026. Based on a leaked internal document shared on Reddit and corroborated by prior FCC filings and render leaks from March 2026.
