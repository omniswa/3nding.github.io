Galaxy Z Fold 8: Release Date, Price, and What's New

July 11, 2026 8-Minute Read

The Galaxy Z Fold 8 isn’t really one phone this year. That’s the part most headlines bury.

Samsung has confirmed its Galaxy Unpacked event for July 22, 2026, held in London at 2 p.m. British Summer Time. That’s the answer to the question that brought you here. Pre-orders should open the same day, with phones shipping roughly two weeks later, so plan on having one in hand by early August 2026.

Here’s the twist. Samsung is splitting its book-style foldable into two separate devices for the first time ever. One is called the Galaxy Z Fold 8. The other is the Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra. They are not the same phone with different storage tiers. They’re built for different people.

Why Samsung Suddenly Needs Two Fold Phones Instead of One

For eight years, “Galaxy Z Fold” meant one shape: tall, narrow when folded, book-like when open. That’s changing because the market changed under Samsung’s feet.

Apple is reportedly bringing its first foldable iPhone this September, and leaks point to a wide, near-4:3 design rather than the tall shape Samsung has used since 2019. A single-product response would leave Samsung exposed on one side of that fight. So instead, Samsung built two.

The Galaxy Z Fold 8 (the wide model, previously nicknamed “Fold 8 Wide” during the leak cycle) is short and broad, closer to a small tablet than a phone. Think media consumption and split-screen multitasking as the priority. The Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra keeps the familiar tall, narrow shape of the Fold 7, just refined. This is the one for people who want the biggest zoom camera and the most phone-like folded footprint.

A Bluetooth SIG certification filing reportedly locked in these names late in development, which is why you’ll see older articles calling the wide model “Fold 8 Wide.” At retail, it’s expected to just say Galaxy Z Fold 8 on the box. No “Wide” suffix. That’s a naming change that trips people up, so it’s worth remembering before you walk into a store.

The Wide Model Is Built for a Fight Apple Hasn’t Shown Up To Yet

Here’s the strategic part, and it’s not subtle. Samsung’s July launch date puts the new Fold 8 on shelves roughly two months before Apple’s rumored September foldable iPhone event. That’s not an accident. It’s a head start.

The wide Fold 8 reportedly carries a 7.6-inch LTPO OLED inner display in a 4:3 aspect ratio, paired with a 5.4-inch cover screen. Both panels are expected to run variable refresh rates from 1Hz to 120Hz, which matters because it’s the difference between a phone that sips battery while you read and one that burns it needlessly. The weight is rumored around 200 grams, and the rear camera is a dual 50MP setup without a telephoto lens. No zoom camera is a real tradeoff. If you photograph kids’ soccer games from the sideline, this isn’t your model. If you photograph documents, screenshots, and split-screen spreadsheets, the missing zoom lens won’t cost you anything.

The Fold 8 Ultra, by contrast, is reportedly just 4.5mm thick when unfolded. That’s only 0.3mm thicker than the Fold 7. Same square corners, same flat sides, same camera bump. Samsung isn’t reinventing this shape. It’s polishing it.

The Crease Problem Might Finally Be Solved (Mostly)

Every foldable phone has the same tell: a visible line down the middle of the screen where it bends. Samsung has chased a “creaseless” display since the original Fold in 2019, and multiple leakers now claim this is the year it gets meaningfully better.

Ice Universe, a leaker with a solid track record on Samsung hardware, reports both 2026 Fold devices will match the crease quality of the Oppo Find N6, which currently sets the bar in this category. That’s a real claim because Oppo’s crease is genuinely hard to notice in daily use, not marketing language dressed up as a spec.

There’s a wrinkle, though, and it favors the wide model specifically. Reports suggest the wide Fold 8 uses a thicker ultra-thin glass layer, around 60 micrometers compared to roughly 45 micrometers on the Ultra. Thicker glass at the fold point tends to distribute stress more evenly, which is why some outlets expect the wide model’s crease to be the less visible of the two. Nothing here is confirmed by Samsung. Treat it as a strong rumor, not a settled fact, until the phone is in reviewers’ hands.

Battery and Charging Finally Catch Up to the Rest of the Industry

Samsung foldables have lagged badly on charging speed. The Fold 7 topped out at 25W wired charging, a number that felt outdated the day it shipped, because plenty of $400 Android phones charge faster.

The Fold 8 lineup is expected to fix this with 45W wired charging, nearly double the old ceiling. Paired with a battery bump from 4,400 mAh on the Fold 7 to a rumored 5,000 mAh, the same cell reportedly used in the Galaxy S26 Ultra, that’s a legitimate quality-of-life upgrade rather than a spec-sheet footnote. Faster charging matters more on a foldable than a regular phone, because these devices burn through battery faster running two active displays.

Gemini Intelligence Debuts Here Before Any Other Android Phone

This is the detail with the most staying power, even though it won’t show up in a store display.

Google’s Gemini Intelligence, an AI layer that can handle multi-step tasks across multiple apps without you manually switching between them, is reportedly launching first on the Fold 8 and Fold 8 Ultra. Not the Pixel line. Not the Galaxy S26. The foldables get it first.

That’s a meaningful exclusive if it holds, and it also makes practical sense. A large, split-screen-capable display is exactly where cross-app automation is most useful, since you can watch the AI work across two apps at once instead of trusting it blindly on a single small screen. The catch: this feature reportedly isn’t even in the current One UI 9 beta being tested on Galaxy S26 units, meaning Samsung is holding it back specifically for a commercial debut on the Fold 8. Software that’s held back for a splashy reveal sometimes ships rough. Wait for hands-on reviews before assuming it works as advertised on day one.

What This Will Actually Cost You

Leaked pricing points to $1,999 for the 256GB model, climbing to $2,199 for 512GB and $2,499 for the 1TB tier. A counter-leak from Korean outlet Newspim, later picked up by Forbes, pegs the Korean 512GB price at roughly 3 million won, or about $1,988 domestically, which lines up closely enough with the US number that the $2,199 figure looks locked in rather than inflated for headlines.

Either way, that’s a $150 to $200 increase over the Fold 7. If you’re budget-conscious, that hike alone might be reason enough to sit this generation out, especially since the Fold 7 will likely see price cuts once the Fold 8 ships.

Should You Wait for the Fold 8, or Buy Now?

Wait for the Fold 8 if:

  • You’re on a Fold 5 or Fold 6, since the battery, charging, and crease improvements stack up to a real generational leap rather than a minor refresh.
  • You’ve never owned a foldable and want the newest hinge and display tech before committing.

Skip the wait if:

  • You’re on a tight budget. The $150–$200 price increase makes this Samsung’s most expensive mainstream foldable ever.
  • You already bought a 2025 flagship. The improvements here are meaningful but not urgent enough to abandon a phone you bought last year.
  • You’d rather see Apple’s foldable iPhone before deciding. It arrives in September, only two months after Samsung’s event, and you’ll get a real side-by-side comparison instead of guessing.

A Word on How Solid Any of This Actually Is

The July 22 date and London venue are confirmed by Samsung itself, backed further by FCC filings and BIS certifications that don’t lie about hardware existing. The two-model split, the naming (Fold 8 versus Fold 8 Ultra), and the general shapes are corroborated across enough independent leakers and outlets that they’re safe to treat as near-certain.

Specific numbers, though, like exact battery capacity, exact pricing, and the Gemini Intelligence exclusive, are still leaks. Good leaks, from sources with decent track records, but leaks all the same. Samsung has surprised people before by shipping something slightly different from what the rumor mill converged on. Read this as the clearest available picture two weeks out from launch, not as a spec sheet copied from Samsung’s own press release, because that release doesn’t exist yet.
