TECNO CAMON 50 Ultra 5G: Full Specs Breakdown

May 12, 2026 8-Minute Read

TECNO announced the CAMON 50 Ultra 5G in March 2026, and it landed at a curious intersection: a phone with flagship camera aspirations priced well below $600. The TECNO CAMON 50 Ultra specs reflect that ambition clearly—Sony primary sensor, 3x optical zoom, military-grade durability, and Android 16 out of the box. What’s less obvious from the numbers alone is where the trade-offs live and which specs genuinely pull weight. That’s what this breakdown addresses.

Display: Curved AMOLED With a Few Asterisks

The screen is a 6.78-inch curved AMOLED panel running at 1208 x 2644 resolution—what TECNO markets as “1.5K.” At 429 pixels per inch, sharpness is not a concern at any practical viewing distance.

The display supports a 144Hz refresh rate [Gizmochina] , though real-world behavior matters here: that 144Hz ceiling is limited to specific apps and gaming scenarios [PetaPixel] , not a blanket experience across the OS. Most browsing and general use will run at a lower adaptive rate.

PWM dimming runs at 2160Hz with HDR10 support [GSMArena] . The high PWM frequency matters for users sensitive to screen flicker—lower PWM rates (under 500Hz) are a known trigger for eye fatigue and headaches during extended sessions.

Both the front and back use Gorilla Glass Victus 2 [PetaPixel] , which is a notable upgrade over the standard Gorilla Glass 7i many mid-rangers include only on the back.

One honest note: curved displays aren’t universally loved [PetaPixel] , and the Camon 50 Ultra’s dual-curve means edge touches can occasionally misfire depending on how you grip it.

Chipset: Dimensity 7400 Ultimate Explained

The phone runs on the MediaTek Dimensity 7400 Ultimate (4nm process), configured as an octa-core with four Cortex-A78 performance cores at 2.6GHz and four Cortex-A55 efficiency cores at 2.0GHz, paired with the Mali-G615 MC2 GPU. [GSMArena]

The “Ultimate” designation matters. It’s not just a marketing suffix—the 7400 Ultimate is a tuned variant of the standard 7400, optimized for camera ISP throughput and AI processing. TECNO’s use case here is intentional: the chip was chosen to support multi-frame computational photography without thermal throttling during extended shooting sessions.

Memory options are 8GB or 12GB RAM with 256GB UFS storage. [GSMArena] TECNO also includes Extended RAM, which borrows from the internal storage—up to 16GB on the 8GB model and 24GB on the 12GB variant. [Tecno] Extended RAM doesn’t perform identically to physical RAM, but it meaningfully reduces app-kill behavior during multitasking.

For context: this isn’t a chip you’d choose for heavy sustained gaming or video editing. It handles daily workloads, casual gaming, and camera processing competently—but anyone expecting Snapdragon 8 Elite performance at this price point is looking at the wrong device.

Camera System: Where the Spec Sheet Gets Interesting

This is the part that requires the most unpacking.

Main Camera

The primary lens is a 50MP Sony LYT-700C sensor with a 1/1.56-inch sensor size, f/1.8 aperture, OIS, and PDAF. [Mobolist] The 1/1.56-inch size is the key number—it’s meaningfully larger than the sensors found in most phones under $400, allowing more light per pixel in low-light conditions. The 1.0µm pixel size at base resolution scales to larger effective pixels when binning is applied.

TECNO claims a DXOMARK overall score of 146, which the company says is the highest among smartphones priced under $600—alongside an impressive portrait score of 142. [Gizmochina] DXOMARK scores carry nuance (they weight AI processing heavily), but the portrait number specifically reflects real optical performance in subject separation and bokeh quality.

Telephoto

The 50MP telephoto shoots at a 70mm equivalent (3x optical zoom) using an OmniVision OV50M sensor on a 1/2.88-inch sensor with f/2.2 aperture and PDAF. [PetaPixel]

A 3x optical telephoto at 70mm hits a genuinely useful focal length—portraits, street photography, moderate wildlife. The sensor is smaller than the primary, which is standard practice, but 50MP resolution at 3x gives you real cropping headroom beyond the optical zoom.

Ultrawide

The 8MP ultrawide covers a 14mm equivalent field of view using a GalaxyCore GC08A8 sensor on a 1/4.0-inch chip with f/2.2 aperture and fixed focus. [PetaPixel]

This is where the camera system shows its budget constraints. The ultrawide is clearly the weakest link—fixed focus, a small sensor, and a budget silicon supplier. At 112 degrees, the coverage is wide, but low-light ultrawide shots will expose the performance gap quickly. It’s not a deal-breaker, but it’s a realistic expectation to set.

Front Camera

The 50MP selfie camera uses a Samsung Isocell JN1 sensor at a 20mm-equivalent focal length with f/2.4 aperture on a 1/2.76-inch sensor. [PetaPixel] That sensor size puts it ahead of most front cameras in this price range.

Battery: 6500mAh With a Specific Chemistry

The 6500mAh battery uses silicon-carbon chemistry and supports 45W wired fast charging, with a full 0–100% recharge taking roughly 80 minutes. [PetaPixel]

Silicon-carbon is worth noting specifically. Traditional lithium-ion batteries use graphite anodes; silicon-carbon replaces or supplements that with silicon, which can hold significantly more charge per unit weight. The practical result is higher energy density—meaning a 6500mAh capacity in a thinner, lighter body than older battery technology could achieve. The phone measures 7.4mm thick and weighs 178g [GSMArena] , which is lean for a battery this size.

Real-world endurance holds up well even with heavier camera use, outpacing some more prominent competitors. [PetaPixel] There’s no wireless charging, which is a gap at this price point compared to some rivals.

Build and Durability: Certifications That Actually Mean Something

The CAMON 50 Ultra carries IP68, IP69, and IP69K ratings, meaning it resists dust, submersion up to 2 meters for 30 minutes, and high-pressure water jets. [Gizmochina] IP69K specifically is the automotive-grade standard—it means the phone can handle steam cleaning. Most phones only carry IP68.

The device also holds MIL-STD-810H military certification, covering drop resistance, shock, and physical stress tests. [Gizmochina]

The frame is plastic [GSMArena] , which is the one structural compromise. A plastic frame is lighter and less prone to bending, but it doesn’t transmit the same premium feel as aluminum.

Connectivity

The phone supports Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax), Bluetooth 5.4, and a full satellite positioning suite including GPS, A-GPS, GLONASS, BeiDou, Galileo, and QZSS. [DeviceSpecifications]

NFC enables “Tap to Transfer” between the Camon 50 series and Apple devices [Tecno] —a cross-platform file sharing feature that doesn’t require an app install on either side. USB is Type-C at USB 2.0 speeds [DeviceSpecifications] , which means wired file transfers are slower than USB 3.0 devices. Not a daily problem, but worth knowing before you plan to dump 4K footage.

Software and Long-Term Support

The phone ships with Android 16 and HiOS 16, with three major Android OS upgrades promised. [GSMArena] TECNO has committed to five years of Android security updates [Gizmochina] —a strong commitment for a device at this price, matching the cadence Google and Samsung offer on much more expensive hardware.

Users also get 50GB of TECNO Cloud storage annually for the first three years. [Tecno]

How It Compares: The Real Context

The CAMON 50 Ultra doesn’t try to compete with Snapdragon 8-series flagships—that’s not the fight it entered. The relevant comparisons are phones like the Redmi Note 14 Pro+, Realme 13 Pro+, and Samsung Galaxy A56.

Against that field, the CAMON 50 Ultra’s Sony primary sensor, IP69K water resistance, and 6500mAh silicon-carbon battery are genuine differentiators. The Dimensity 7400 Ultimate is competitive within the segment, though not dominant. The ultrawide camera trails peers that use better ultrawide hardware. And the plastic frame is a concession that phones like the Galaxy A56 avoid with an aluminum build.

Based on the spec sheet, the CAMON 50 Ultra reads more like the successor to the CAMON 40 Pro 5G than a full replacement of the CAMON 40 Premier [GadgetMatch] —a distinction worth understanding if you’re upgrading from a previous CAMON device and expecting a step up in performance ceiling.

Worth Buying?

The TECNO CAMON 50 Ultra 5G makes a specific case: maximum camera hardware at a sub-$600 price, wrapped in a surprisingly durable shell. If photography is the primary use case and you can live with a capable-but-not-blazing chipset, the value is real. The silicon-carbon battery and multi-IP water resistance are genuine advantages that require spending significantly more to match elsewhere.

Where it asks for patience is the ultrawide camera, USB 2.0 speeds, and the absence of wireless charging. None of those are fatal flaws—they’re the price of admission in this segment.
