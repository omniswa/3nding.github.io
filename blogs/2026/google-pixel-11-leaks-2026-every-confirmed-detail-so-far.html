Google Pixel 11 Leaks 2026: Every Confirmed Detail So Far

July 19, 2026 8-Minute Read

Google will unveil the Pixel 11 lineup on August 12, 2026, at a Made by Google event in New York City starting at 6 PM EDT. That part isn’t a leak. Google confirmed the date itself.

Everything else — the four models, the pricing, the RAM cuts, the new Tensor G6 chip — comes from leaks. But this year the leaks are unusually solid, because Google’s own Amazon listings went live early and got scraped before anyone could pull them down. That’s not a small tipster with a source inside a factory. That’s the company’s own retail page, confirming its own product. So treat the numbers below as close to final, not speculative.

Four phones are coming, and the naming finally makes sense

The lineup splits into the Pixel 11, Pixel 11 Pro, Pixel 11 Pro XL, and Pixel 11 Pro Fold. Internally, Google gave them bear-themed codenames — Cubs, Grizzly, Kodiak, and Yogi. That’s cute, but it also tells you something useful: a shared codename family usually means a shared chip and shared software timeline, which lines up with the single-event launch.

Four models feels like a lot until you remember Google has run this exact structure for two years now. The base phone, two Pro sizes, and one fold. Nothing here breaks that pattern.

Pricing leaked, and it’s not the flat increase everyone feared

Here’s what the scraped Amazon listings reportedly show:

  • Pixel 11: $899 for 256GB, $1,019 for 512GB
  • Pixel 11 Pro XL: starts at $1,299 for 256GB
  • Pixel 11 Pro Fold: starts at $1,899

Notice the base Pixel 11 no longer offers 128GB storage. Google appears to be pushing everyone up to 256GB as the entry point. That’s good news if you hated the old 128GB tier, since it was always too small for anyone shooting 4K video. It’s less good news for your wallet, since a bigger baseline storage tier at the same headline price often just means the actual profit margin moved, not that you got a free upgrade.

European leaks point to roughly €100 more than the Pixel 10 Pro. That’s consistent with what’s happening industry-wide. Samsung already raised the Galaxy S26 price and its own mobile COO told The Verge memory chip shortages made a “significant contribution” to that hike. AI data centers are buying up global memory supply right now, and phones compete for the same chips. Google seems to be handling that pressure differently than Samsung, though, which brings us to the more uncomfortable leak.

The base Pixel 11 might ship with 8GB of RAM, and that’s a real problem

Every Pixel 10 model shipped with a flat 12GB of RAM. Leaks now say the standard Pixel 11 could drop to just 8GB. Some leaked spec sheets show 8/12GB as configurable options, which suggests Google hasn’t locked this down yet, or is testing regional variants.

This matters more than a typical spec bump because of timing. Google confirmed Gemini Intelligence as this generation’s headline AI system back in May 2026. That system needs at least 12GB of RAM and Gemini Nano v3 to run fully on-device, according to leaked technical documentation. If the base Pixel 11 actually ships with 8GB, you’d be buying a phone that can’t fully run the feature Google is building its entire marketing campaign around.

Google is choosing to hold the price and cut the spec instead of raising the price and keeping the spec, which is the opposite of what Samsung just did with the Galaxy S26. That’s a real trade-off, not a marketing footnote. If you care about on-device AI running at full capability on day one, don’t buy the entry-level Pixel 11 without checking the final RAM configuration when it launches.

Tensor G6 finally kills the modem problem, on paper

The new chip is Google’s first built on a 2nm process, manufactured by TSMC rather than Samsung’s foundry. That’s a meaningful jump. A smaller node generally means better efficiency, which for a phone translates directly into battery life and heat management — the two things Pixel phones have struggled with for years.

The bigger change is the modem. Tensor G6 reportedly pairs with a MediaTek M90 modem, replacing the Samsung Exynos modem Google has used since Tensor launched. Exynos modems have been a recurring complaint in Pixel reviews — spotty 5G handoffs, weaker signal in marginal coverage areas. If MediaTek’s modem performs the way leaks suggest, this is the single upgrade most likely to actually change how the phone feels day-to-day, more than any camera spec will.

The CPU configuration is a 1+4+2 setup using newer ARM C1 cores, with a PowerVR C-Series GPU rather than ARM’s newest Immortalis design. That GPU choice means gaming performance will likely still trail Samsung’s Galaxy S26 and Apple’s iPhone lineup. Google has consistently prioritized AI and camera processing over raw gaming horsepower with Tensor chips, and nothing in these leaks suggests that’s changing.

Camera hardware is getting its first real sensor refresh in years

Leaks point to a new main camera sensor, codenamed “chemosh,” landing across the Pixel 11, Pro, and Pro Fold. It’s believed to be 50MP. That may not sound dramatic, but Google has been using variations of the same core sensor for multiple generations, so a genuine new sensor is bigger news than the megapixel count implies.

The Pixel 11 Pro also reportedly gets a new telephoto sensor alongside the new main camera. Combined with what leakers describe as a “Pixel Glow” feature — details are still thin on what this actually does — Google appears to be treating computational photography as its main battleground again, the same way it has since the original Pixel.

Colors are basically confirmed, and outlets don’t fully agree on names

Different leaked listings show slightly different names for the same shades, which usually happens when a product goes through regional naming variants before launch. One set of Amazon listings named Obsidian, Hibiscus, and Pistachio for the base Pixel 11. Another outlet’s leaked wallpapers and listings referenced Fuchsia, Moss, and Midnight instead.

These are probably the same three colors under different regional or preliminary naming, not six separate options. Google has done this before — a shade gets an internal or early-market name that changes by the time it reaches the final product page. Don’t read too much into the discrepancy. Do expect a pink, a green, and a dark neutral, whatever they end up being called.

A Pixel Tag accessory might finally be real

The same leaked Amazon listings that revealed the colorways also mentioned a mysterious Pixel Tag accessory. Google has been rumored to be building an item tracker to compete with Apple’s AirTag and Samsung’s SmartTag for years without ever shipping one. If it shows up in the same retail listings as the phones themselves, that’s a stronger signal than the usual patent-filing rumor. Retail listings mean a SKU exists, and a SKU means someone at Google has already priced it for shelves.

The Pixel 11 Pro Fold: expect a smaller battery and a thinner body

This is the one leak that should make current Pixel 10 Pro Fold owners pause before assuming next year’s model is a clean upgrade. The Pro Fold’s battery is leaked at somewhere between 4,750mAh and 4,800mAh, down from the Pixel 10 Pro Fold’s 5,000mAh.

That’s a real reduction, not a rounding difference. Google appears to be betting that the Tensor G6’s 2nm efficiency gains will offset the smaller cell and produce equal or better battery life. TSMC’s 2nm process is estimated to deliver roughly 15% better power efficiency than the previous node. Whether that math actually works out in daily use is the one thing no leak can tell you, since efficiency gains on paper don’t always survive contact with a folding display that has to power two screens.

The Pro Fold is also expected to get the same all-glass camera bar redesign and thinner body rumored for the standard Pro XL, while keeping the same fundamental camera hardware limitations that reviewers have criticized in past Fold models. If camera quality on the Fold specifically is your priority, these leaks suggest the improvement will be incremental, not the generational leap other Pixel 11 models are getting.

Should you wait for the Pixel 11, or buy a Pixel 10 now?

If you specifically want Gemini Intelligence running at full on-device capability, wait and confirm the final RAM spec on the base Pixel 11 before buying anything — the 8GB leak is the single detail worth waiting on. If you want the best modem Google has shipped in years, the switch away from Exynos is a genuine reason to wait rather than buy a discounted Pixel 10 today. But if camera hardware and raw gaming performance are what you care about most, the improvements here are real but not dramatic enough to make a current Pixel 10 feel obsolete.

Nothing here is officially confirmed by Google beyond the August 12 event date itself. Treat every spec above as “very likely, pending the actual keynote,” and expect the final RAM and battery numbers specifically to be the two figures most likely to shift between now and launch day.
