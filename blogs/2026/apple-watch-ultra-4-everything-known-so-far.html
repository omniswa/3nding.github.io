Apple Watch Ultra 4: Everything Known So Far

June 17, 2026 9-Minute Read

Most people buying a $800 smartwatch assume the biggest number means the biggest upgrade. With the Apple Watch Ultra, that assumption has been wrong twice in a row — and 2026 might finally be the year it becomes true.

The Ultra 4 is expected to launch in September 2026. Most leaks and analyst reports point to evolutionary design refinements rather than a sweeping overhaul, but the cumulative weight of those refinements is more significant than any single previous Ultra generation. [International Business Times] That distinction matters if you’re deciding whether to buy the Ultra 3 now or hold out.

The Ultra Line Has Been Coasting — Until Now

The original Apple Watch Ultra, launched in 2022, was genuinely surprising. Titanium shell. OLED built for direct sunlight. Dual-frequency GPS. A battery that lasted two days. It was the first Apple Watch that felt purpose-built for people who actually needed it to survive something.

Then came the Ultra 2. Then the Ultra 3. Both were refinements so subtle that buyers upgrading from the first generation barely noticed. The Ultra 3 was only a minor jump from the Ultra 2 — which means Ultra 2 owners have been sitting on a perfectly functional watch for two generations with no compelling reason to spend $800 again. [The Gadgeteer]

The Ultra 4 changes that math. Here’s how.

A 15% Thinner Case Sounds Small — It Isn’t

Multiple leaks indicate the Ultra 4 will be approximately 10–15% slimmer in profile compared to its predecessor. [MSN] The Ultra 3 sits at 12mm deep. Shave 15% off that and you get closer to 10mm — which sounds like a trivial technical footnote until you’ve worn a 49mm titanium disc through airport security, underwater, or under a wetsuit.

The case stays at 49mm titanium, Apple’s proven material for ruggedness at minimal weight. The change in depth makes a real impact in daily life, sleeping, and under a sleeve. [Bandstil] Wrist fatigue on multi-day events is cumulative. Grams and millimeters compound.

The display situation is less settled. Earlier rumors suggested a significant redesign tied to MicroLED display technology. However, since Apple has delayed MicroLED plans, the Ultra 4 is expected to keep an OLED display. Instead of a major overhaul, current expectations suggest a slightly thinner titanium case with an edge-to-edge aesthetic. [The Mac Observer]

A nearly bezel-less display and a rumored brightness of 3,500 nits on a tandem OLED panel [MSN] would make it easier to read during high-altitude glare or direct equatorial sun — two scenarios where the Ultra’s existing 3,000-nit panel already beats every competitor.

The S11 Chip Is the Engine for Everything Else

Every meaningful rumor about the Ultra 4 traces back, directly or indirectly, to the S11 chip. This is why the other upgrades are credible — not because Apple said so, but because the hardware arithmetic supports them.

The S11 chip’s 2nm architecture could bring both AI-driven features and significant energy savings, extending battery life without increasing size. [MSN] Smaller process nodes mean transistors consume less power at the same performance level. That’s not marketing language — it’s the same mechanism that made the A17 Pro measurably more efficient than the A16.

Thanks to the energy-efficient S11 chip, rumors suggest the device could offer nearly 48 hours of regular use on a single charge. [Geeky Gadgets] The Apple Watch Ultra 3 already delivers around 42 hours in standard use and up to 72 hours in Low-Power Mode [phonearena] , so even a moderate efficiency gain from a new process node moves the needle on multi-day expedition use.

Digitimes reports that power efficiency will improve significantly on the new Ultra model [9to5Mac] — and that’s the source that also broke accurate information on the Ultra 3’s sensor changes.

Eight Sensors Instead of Four — Why That Actually Matters

Supply chain publication DigiTimes reports that the Apple Watch Ultra 4 will double the number of sensor components compared to the current model. This suggests Apple is expanding its hardware-based measurement capabilities rather than relying heavily on algorithmic estimation. [The Mac Observer]

That last clause is the important part. Most wearable health data today is software-derived. Your Apple Watch doesn’t directly measure your blood oxygen so much as it takes an optical reading and runs it through an algorithm trained on population-level data. More physical sensors mean more raw signal — and more raw signal means the algorithm has more to work with, which means fewer false readings and better accuracy at the edges (altitude, cold water, high-intensity intervals) where a single optical sensor struggles most.

Key improvements are expected in heart rate monitoring during high-intensity workouts, blood oxygen accuracy especially at altitude, irregular heart rhythm detection, and sleep tracking precision — enabled partly by the slimmer, more comfortable form factor that improves overnight wrist contact. [Smartwatch Insight]

Blood glucose monitoring, which Apple has been chasing for years, is not part of this release. Apple continues to work on blood glucose monitoring, but most reports agree this feature will not arrive with the Ultra 4. Development continues, yet 2026 does not appear to be the year for it. [The Mac Observer] Manage expectations accordingly.

Touch ID on a Watch Is a Bigger Deal Than It Sounds

One of the most interesting developments comes from developer Filipe Espósito, who discovered references to “AppleMesa” inside leaked firmware for 2026 Apple Watch models. AppleMesa is Apple’s long-standing internal codename for Touch ID. The presence of this code strongly suggests Apple is testing biometric authentication directly on the watch. [The Mac Observer]

Why does this matter more than it first appears? The Apple Watch currently unlocks via passcode or by staying in wrist-detection contact with your skin. Wrist detection breaks the moment the watch shifts slightly or you take it off briefly — which happens constantly during workouts, water sports, and medical procedures. Every time it breaks, you re-enter a six-digit code on a 49mm display with wet or gloved fingers.

A physical fingerprint sensor would bypass all of that. It would also make Apple Pay faster on the wrist, which matters if you use transit cards or contactless payments regularly. [The Gadgeteer]

The caveat is real, though. A May 2026 report from MacRumors cited a prominent leaker who pushed back directly, arguing that Apple is prioritizing battery capacity over biometrics and that Touch ID is unlikely to make the cut this generation. [Memeburn] Firmware references are evidence of testing. They are not a product announcement.

Blood Pressure Monitoring Is the Health Story for 2026

The existing feature on Apple Watch Series 9 and newer already uses the optical heart sensor to analyze blood vessel responses over a 30-day period. The new implementation for the Ultra 4 appears to represent a more refined and clinically validated version of that underlying technology, potentially enabled by the doubled sensor array. [Memeburn]

For context, this is not a futuristic concept. Samsung has been in this space for years. Samsung Galaxy Watch models have offered blood pressure monitoring outside the United States since 2020, and the feature finally became available to U.S. users on Galaxy Watch 4 and newer in March 2026. [Memeburn] The FDA has been the chokepoint. Apple getting a clinically validated hypertension alert — not just a notification, but a reading accurate enough to pass regulatory review — would be the most significant health upgrade the Apple Watch has seen since ECG.

The feature is reportedly still under FDA review. [Memeburn] That review may or may not clear in time for a September launch. If it does, the Ultra 4 becomes a medical device in a $900 titanium case.

Price and Release: What Analysts Agree On

Analysts expect a starting price of around $899 — stable compared to the Ultra 2 and Ultra 3. Apple has not raised the price on its Ultra models to date. [Bandstil] That consistency is itself unusual in the current hardware market, and it’s a function of Apple treating the Ultra as a halo product rather than a volume driver.

The Apple Watch Ultra 4 is expected to be announced at Apple’s fall event in early September 2026, with retail availability approximately 7 to 10 days after the announcement. This follows Apple’s consistent launch pattern for the Ultra line. [Memeburn]

Who Should Buy Now and Who Should Wait

This is the question everyone is actually asking, so here’s a direct answer organized by situation.

Buy the Ultra 3 now if you’ve never owned an Ultra and need one before September. The Ultra 3 has satellite messaging, a 3,000-nit display, 42-hour battery, and titanium construction. At $699 — the lowest the Ultra line has ever been — it’s a legitimate value case. [The Gadgeteer] It will receive watchOS updates for years.

Wait for the Ultra 4 if you own an Ultra 2 or earlier. The Ultra 3 was only a minor jump from the Ultra 2, which means the Ultra 4 will be the first generation that actually feels like a real upgrade for you. [The Gadgeteer] The doubled sensor array, thinner case, and S11 efficiency gains stack into something genuinely different from what you already have.

Wait for the Ultra 4 if blood pressure monitoring or the expanded sensor array is your primary motivation. The Ultra 4 is expected to double the number of sensor components compared to the Ultra 3, with eight sensors arranged in a ring pattern on the back of the device. [Memeburn] That hardware isn’t coming to the Ultra 3 via software update.

Buy the Ultra 3 now if Touch ID is your only reason to wait. That feature is unconfirmed and actively disputed by credible sources. Betting three months of watch ownership on firmware code references is a poor trade.

The Honest Summary

The Ultra 4 is shaping up to be the first Apple Watch Ultra that justifies the “generation” label rather than the “revision” label. A new chip architecture, double the health sensors, a measurably thinner case, and possible blood pressure monitoring under FDA review — that’s a meaningful package.

Nothing here is confirmed. Apple hasn’t said a word. September will tell the whole story.

But if you’re standing in an Apple Store right now with your credit card out, the question to ask yourself is simple: are you in a hurry? If not, three months of patience is probably worth it.
