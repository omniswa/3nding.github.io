Xbox Series X25: Everything You Need to Know

June 8, 2026 6-Minute Read

Twenty-five years in, Microsoft’s most honest anniversary move isn’t a new console. It’s the old one in a see-through shell.

The Xbox Series X25 Limited Edition and the Xbox Wireless Controller X25 Special Edition were revealed at the Xbox Games Showcase on June 7, 2026. [XBOX Wire] If you came here for the short answer: this is a cosmetic special edition of the existing Xbox Series X, dressed in translucent OG Green, launching this November with no price confirmed yet.

The Design Is Doing All the Heavy Lifting Here

The design pulls directly from the original Xbox, right down to the iconic “X” lighting up green when you power the console on — a detail longtime fans will recognize immediately, and a smarter tribute than just slapping a logo on a colored faceplate. [TweakTown]

That choice is intentional and earned. The original Xbox, launched on November 15, 2001, was defined by that aggressive green-and-black color language. It was almost garish. It was absolutely unforgettable. Bringing translucency into it — so you can see the hardware inside — adds a layer that a flat paint job never could. You’re not just looking at green plastic. You’re looking through it.

There’s a 25th Anniversary logo on the front of the console, and the X lights up in the green glow that was recently teased with the new Xbox logo. It’s the same internal hardware as the standard 1TB Xbox Series X, but in translucent green so you can see the internal components. [Windows Central]

Microsoft says you’ll also find hidden surprises throughout the design — described as a thank you to the community. [XBOX Wire] No specifics have been confirmed yet. That’s a deliberate slow reveal, designed to reward people who actually buy and hold the hardware.

What the Controller Gets Right

The controller might be the better purchase here — both in terms of value and craftsmanship.

From the original ABXY colors to the timeless green, every detail calls back to the beginning, including the bumpers honoring the original black and white buttons on the original “Duke” controller. The back case and battery door are fully transparent, revealing the classic Xbox logo. [CGMagazine]

The Duke controller — the enormous, polarizing original Xbox pad — had black and white face buttons that got quietly dropped in later revisions. Putting them back as bumper tributes is exactly the kind of detail that separates a thoughtful limited edition from a cash grab. It’s not nostalgia for nostalgia’s sake. It’s a specific acknowledgment that the community remembers, and that Microsoft remembers that they remember.

The Xbox Wireless Controller X25 Special Edition is coming in October 2026, with the console bundle arriving in November. [ResetEra] That staggered release means you can grab the controller first, which makes sense if you already own a Series X and don’t need the whole package.

The Specs Are Not the Point

The console carries 1TB of storage, and under the hood, nothing has changed. This is still a standard Xbox Series X — you’re paying for the look, not a hardware bump. [TweakTown]

That’s the honest reality, and it’s worth saying plainly. If you’re hoping for a performance upgrade tied to this anniversary release, there isn’t one. The same custom AMD Zen 2 CPU, the same RDNA 2-based GPU, the same 12 teraflops of compute performance that shipped with the Series X back in 2020.

What you’re actually buying is continuity — the physical argument that the Xbox as a platform has a history worth displaying on your shelf. For a lot of people, that’s enough. For others, it isn’t. Both reactions are valid.

Pricing Is the Open Question Nobody Can Answer Yet

Pricing was not announced, but it’s expected the system will cost a premium given its special design and the current global macroeconomic environment driving up prices on most products. [GameSpot]

The standard Xbox Series X currently retails at $649.99 in the US. Limited edition consoles almost always carry a markup — often $50 to $100 above the base model, sometimes more for collector-tier hardware. Microsoft says more information on pricing and pre-order availability will come “soon.” [MobileSyrup]

Watch the major retailers. When pre-orders open, they tend to open fast and sell out faster for hardware like this.

Why November Specifically

The original Xbox launched on November 21, 2001 in North America. The X25 name makes the math explicit: 2001 + 25 = 2026. [Bitsfrombytes] Microsoft is timing the release to land in the same month the brand was born. That’s not coincidence — it’s the entire narrative framework of the campaign.

Xbox has also confirmed that its game-preservation team is working to bring back select iconic retro titles in 2026 in “entirely new ways,” using the same model as past backward-compatibility upgrades like Auto HDR and FPS Boost — modernizing classics without changing their core identity. [notebookcheck]

The hardware and the software are meant to work together as a single statement. The X25 console is the physical anchor. The revived catalog is the content reason to power it on.

What This Says About Xbox’s Next Move

The timing of this release implies that Project Helix — Xbox’s next-generation console — is unlikely to arrive before 2027 at the earliest. [TweakTown] You don’t drop a $600-plus limited edition of existing hardware into the holiday window if a full generational leap is coming six months later. That would cannibalize the market and confuse the message.

The X25 is a celebration, yes. It’s also a holding pattern made as beautiful as possible.

Xbox is no longer treating exclusivity as a competitive weapon — it’s treating its IP catalog as a revenue-generating library. [Bitsfrombytes] Halo: Campaign Evolved, announced alongside the X25, ships on PlayStation 5 for the first time in franchise history. The hardware is limited edition. The games are now everywhere. That’s the strategic shift hiding behind the translucent green shell.

Should You Buy It

If you’re a longtime Xbox fan who remembers watching that first green “X” flash on screen in 2001, this is one of the more thoughtful pieces of anniversary hardware the company has ever built. The design decisions are specific, the callbacks are earned, and the physical object — see-through shell, glowing X, Duke-tribute bumpers — is genuinely striking.

If you’re waiting for a next-gen performance leap, this isn’t it. Wait.

The anniversary console bundle launches in November 2026 in select markets, with pricing and pre-order information still to come. [Beebom] Keep an eye on Xbox Wire and major retailers for the moment that window opens. For a limited edition with this much visibility, the pre-order window will be short.
