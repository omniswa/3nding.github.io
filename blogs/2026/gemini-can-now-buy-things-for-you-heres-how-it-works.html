Gemini Can Now Buy Things For You. Here's How It Works

July 2, 2026 8-Minute Read

Gemini isn’t just answering shopping questions anymore. It’s finishing the transaction.

Ask it for something specific — floral leggings, a compatible power supply for a custom PC build, a birthday gift under fifty dollars — and it can search, compare, and now actually complete the purchase without you opening a retailer’s website. That last part is the shift. For years, AI shopping tools stopped at recommendations. You still had to click through, log in, and check out somewhere else. Gemini closes that gap.

The Short Answer: What Changed and Why It Matters

The core mechanism behind this is something called the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), an open standard Google co-developed with retailers like Target, Shopify, Etsy, Walmart, and Wayfair. UCP establishes a common language for agents and systems to operate together across consumer surfaces, businesses, and payment providers, so instead of requiring a unique connection for every individual agent, it lets all agents interact with merchant systems the same way. [Google]

Think of it like a universal translator sitting between Gemini and a store’s checkout system. Before UCP, every retailer’s checkout flow was its own dialect: different forms, different APIs, different login requirements. Gemini would’ve needed a custom integration for each one. UCP standardizes that handshake, so Gemini can complete a purchase on Wayfair the same way it completes one on Target, without either company rebuilding its backend from scratch.

That’s why checkout inside Gemini rolled out fast once UCP launched. UCP was co-developed by Google and Shopify starting in January 2026, and it gives AI agents a common language to interact with merchant checkout systems, handling product selection, payment, and confirmation without requiring a human to navigate a website. [Shine Dezign Infonet]

How Gemini Actually Finds Products (It’s Not a New Search Engine)

Here’s the part people get wrong. Gemini isn’t crawling the web fresh every time you ask about leggings. It’s pulling from Google’s existing Shopping Graph, a structured database Google has been building for over a decade.

The mechanism matters here because it explains why some products show up polished and others barely appear at all. The Shopping Graph holds over 50 billion product listings, continuously updated and interpreted by Gemini models. [Shine Dezign Infonet] A listing gets pulled into a Gemini answer based on how complete and structured its data is — things like GTINs, size, color, and material attributes. Sparse or messy feeds get skipped. Rich ones get surfaced, compared, and recommended.

This is why retailers are scrambling to fix their product feeds right now. It’s not an SEO problem anymore. It’s a data-completeness problem. Gemini can’t reason about a product it can’t parse.

What Buying Something Actually Looks Like Inside the Chat

Say you tell Gemini: “I’m getting into working out, help me find floral leggings, light color, under $40.” You’d see different options to consider and purchase without leaving the chat, a capability that comes from UCP creating a common language for AI agents and merchant systems to work together. [Target Corporation]

That flow only works because retailers opted in. Target confirmed guests will be able to shop and purchase Target products directly in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Google Search using this functionality. [Target Corporation] Not every retailer supports it yet. If a store hasn’t integrated UCP, Gemini can still describe and recommend its products, but it’ll hand you off to the site to finish buying. That distinction — recommend versus transact — is the single most useful thing to check before assuming any product is one-click purchasable.

The Part Everyone Worries About: Who’s Actually Paying, and Can It Go Wrong

Handing an AI agent your payment method sounds risky, and the risk is real enough that Google built a separate protocol just to contain it. It’s called AP2, the Agent Payments Protocol.

The mechanism here is worth understanding because it’s the actual safeguard, not just a policy promise. AP2 creates a transparent, verifiable link between you, the merchant, and the payment processor, using privacy-preserving technology to keep your data safe. Tamper-proof digital mandates ensure the agent is always acting on your behalf, giving you a permanent digital paper trail. [Google]

That paper trail matters more than it sounds. If a return dispute happens, you and the merchant are looking at the same record [Google] — the mandate acts like a notarized instruction, not a guess about what you meant. And critically, every transaction still requires explicit shopper confirmation. [Talon.One] Gemini can prep the cart, apply the loyalty discount, flag the price drop. It cannot silently buy something on your behalf without you tapping confirm.

Price tracking is where this gets genuinely useful rather than just novel. You tell Gemini a target price, and it monitors continuously in the background. Price tracking can trigger a “buy for me” option, and the agent monitors pricing and can act when conditions are met, though every purchase still requires explicit shopper confirmation. [Talon.One] That’s the mechanism replacing the old habit of manually rechecking a product page every few days hoping for a sale.

Why Your Cart Now “Thinks” About What You’re Buying

Google’s newer Universal Cart feature goes a step further than checkout. It reasons about the relationship between items, not just their price.

The moment you add a product to your cart, it gets to work in the background, finding deals and price drops, giving insights on price history, and alerting you when an item is back in stock. It all runs on Gemini models, so the cart gets smarter as the models improve. [Google]

The compatibility-checking is the clearest example of why this matters practically. If you’re building a custom PC and add parts from several retailers, the cart proactively flags product incompatibilities and suggests alternatives. [Google] The mechanism is straightforward once you see it: Gemini cross-references product specs against each other using the same structured data it pulled from the Shopping Graph in the first place. It’s not magic. It’s just doing the spec-sheet comparison you’d normally do manually across ten open tabs.

The Feature Nobody Expected: Gemini Calling Stores For You

One of the odder additions doesn’t involve checkout at all. It involves phone calls.

This feature is powered by Duplex technology with a Gemini model upgrade that identifies the best stores to call, suggests helpful questions to ask based on the product you want, and summarizes the conversation into key takeaways. [Google] If you need something today and want to confirm a local store actually has it in stock — a task that used to mean being on hold for ten minutes — Gemini places the call and reports back.

This only works because Duplex’s voice model can hold a real-time conversation convincingly enough that store employees answer questions normally. It’s the same underlying tech Google demoed years ago for restaurant reservations, repurposed for inventory checks.

Where This Still Breaks Down (Be Honest With Yourself Here)

Not every merchant supports agentic checkout yet, and that’s the biggest practical limitation right now. Agentic checkout rolled out starting with eligible merchants like Wayfair, Chewy, Quince, and select Shopify merchants in the U.S., with more expected to follow. [Google] If your preferred store isn’t on that list, Gemini’s usefulness caps out at recommendation and comparison. You’ll still be redirected to check out manually.

There’s also a structural shift happening underneath all of this that’s easy to miss if you’re only thinking about convenience. Historically the purchase journey moved across surfaces: discover on Google, consider on the retailer’s website, buy at checkout. Agentic commerce collapses that into a single flow. [Shine Dezign Infonet] That’s good for you as a shopper. It’s a genuine business problem for retailers who built their entire funnel around getting you onto their site, because now the transaction can finish before you ever land there.

What To Actually Do With This Right Now

If you’re a shopper, three things are worth doing today.

  • Check whether Gemini’s shopping features are live in your region — the rollout is U.S.-first and expanding gradually
  • Set a price-tracking alert on anything you’re on the fence about, rather than manually rechecking pages
  • Confirm your Google Pay details are current, since that’s the payment rail behind agentic checkout

If you’re evaluating this as a retailer or seller, the calculus is different. Product feed quality isn’t a nice-to-have anymore — it’s the mechanism by which Gemini decides whether your product exists at all in these answers. A listing missing structured attributes doesn’t get penalized. It just doesn’t get seen.
