iOS 27: Every Real Feature That Matters

June 13, 2026 8-Minute Read

Most people still think of a new iOS as a bag of features dropped on top of the old one. iOS 27 is more honest than that. It’s a quiet admission that the last two years of Apple software shipped half-finished—and a deliberate attempt to make things right before anyone noticed the crack widened any further.

iOS 27 is what Apple internally calls a “Snow Leopard-style” update—fixing the underlying machinery rather than bolting on surface-level additions. [Digital Trends] That framing matters. Snow Leopard, released in 2009, is the benchmark for what Apple does when the foundation needs shoring up. It’s a sign the company knows something broke.

For the past two years, Apple had been racing to catch up in AI while frustrations with its core software quietly added up: a design overhaul users resented, a search function that barely worked, and a file-sharing feature that routinely failed. [TechCrunch] iOS 27 is the reckoning.

The Speed Numbers Are Real—Here’s Why

Speed claims from any company are easy to dismiss. These are harder to wave off.

Apple’s own testing found up to 30% faster app launches, up to 70% quicker photo loading times after shooting, and up to 80% faster AirDrop transfers. [Digital Trends] That testing was conducted in April and May 2026 using real production hardware—iPhone 11 Pro Max and iPhone 15—with a pre-release iOS 27 build, measured after many cycles of device usage. [Apple]

The mechanism is a revamped CPU scheduler. A CPU scheduler decides which tasks get processor time and in what order. The old one was built for a different generation of Apple chips and hadn’t been meaningfully overhauled as the silicon changed underneath it. iOS 27 introduces a new CPU scheduler designed to improve efficiency [Blog do iPhone] , meaning the chip does the same work with fewer wasted cycles—which is why the gains show up everywhere from app launches to file transfers, not just in one isolated benchmark.

And unlike most iOS releases, Apple confirmed that all devices running iOS 26 will be able to install iOS 27. [Tom’s Guide] Not a single device cut. That’s deliberate. The speed work benefits even older hardware because the scheduler change is architectural, not chip-specific.

Siri Got a Rebuild, Not a Patch—and Google Is Underneath It

This is the part of iOS 27 that will age the most visibly—in either direction.

Apple has rebuilt Siri into what it’s calling “Siri AI,” powered by Google Gemini under the hood, with a standalone app, on-screen awareness, and the ability to access Messages, Mail, Photos, and on-screen content in real-time without switching apps. [TechCrunch]

That last part is the fundamental shift. The old Siri was a request-response system with essentially no memory and no view into what was on your screen. It could tell you the weather. It could not help you finish what you were already doing. WWDC demos showed the new Siri surfacing specific photos with filtered faces without opening the Photos app, and building context from what was visible on screen in real time. [Digital Trends]

Apple’s Foundation Models run the on-device layer while the top-tier AFM Cloud Pro model—running on Nvidia GPUs in Google’s infrastructure—matches Gemini Frontier quality. [Memeburn] So the assistant that answers you quickly and privately is a local model. The one that handles complex multi-step requests hands off to the cloud. You don’t choose. The system decides based on the task.

There is also a new “Search or Ask” interface: swiping down from the top center launches a panel where you can run system shortcuts, search your device, or hand off complex queries to third-party chatbots including ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. [Tom’s Guide]

The Gemini partnership will raise questions Apple hasn’t fully answered yet. But practically, for users: the output quality should be substantially better than the previous Siri on any task requiring reasoning, multi-step logic, or contextual memory. Short. That’s the useful takeaway.

Liquid Glass Finally Has an Off Switch

iOS 26 introduced Liquid Glass—Apple’s translucent, frosted-glass visual design—and many users found it difficult to read in bright light. [Memeburn]

Apple’s solution in iOS 27 is not to remove the design language. It’s to give users control over how much of it they see. There is now a transparency slider, a sliding scale that gives users a great deal more control over what Liquid Glass looks like across iOS and the rest of the Apple ecosystem—previously the only option was an accessibility toggle to turn off all transparency effects entirely. [Tom’s Guide]

That distinction matters. A binary switch treats the transparency as a problem to be eliminated. A slider treats it as a design preference to be calibrated. Apple gets to keep its aesthetic decision. Users get to not squint at their phone in sunlight. Both things happen.

Liquid Glass is also getting a new layered effect in iOS 27 that makes app icons more striking with an almost-3D appearance. [Geeky Gadgets]

The Small Things Apple Didn’t Mention—But Should Have

Alarms, timers, and system sounds now have independent volume controls. [Blog do iPhone] That one sentence represents years of annoyance for millions of people. Previously, those sounds shared a single volume setting. Turning down your ringer at night meant your 6 a.m. alarm came in equally quiet. Apple fixed it. No announcement. No fanfare.

When something is copied to the clipboard, the keyboard now automatically displays a quick-paste button, saving time when working with copied text, links, or images. [Blog do iPhone]

Giant widgets can now fill an entire Home Screen page, making it possible to dedicate a full page to apps like Calendar, Reminders, or Home. [Blog do iPhone]

Safari now automatically groups open tabs into distinct topics based on content, and a new “Notify Me” feature monitors specific web pages for changes—alerting you to restocks, price drops, or event updates without you having to check manually. [Fone Arena]

The Shortcuts app can now build automation sequences entirely from natural language descriptions, and the Passwords app can automatically detect weak or compromised credentials and update them natively on your behalf. [Fone Arena]

These aren’t showstoppers. They’re the kind of thing you use every day for the rest of your life once you have them.

Parental Controls Got Their First Real Redesign Since 2018

iOS 27 delivers the most significant expansion of Apple’s family-safety toolkit in eight years. [Tech Times] The old Screen Time system required parents to manually maintain URL blocklists. Motivated kids found workarounds. The architecture was adversarial and exhausting.

The new “Ask to Browse” feature inverts that architecture entirely—no website in Safari is reachable by a child until a parent explicitly approves it. [Tech Times] This is the difference between a wall with holes and a locked door with a request button.

The broader overhaul includes mandatory child accounts for users under 13, flexible Time Allowances that can vary by day of week, App Schedules that set specific windows when apps are accessible, and expanded Communication Safety in Messages and FaceTime. [Apple Headlines]

Screen Time itself has been rebuilt for a simpler at-a-glance view of a child’s device usage, including weekly summaries and the apps used most on any given day. [9to5Mac]

The context here is important: Apple unveiled these changes on the same morning UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer issued demands at London Tech Week for device-level controls preventing children from viewing explicit content across any app. [Tech Times] The timing was not accidental. Whether these controls fully satisfy that regulatory push is a different question—but the direction is clear.

What Your iPhone Actually Gets—Without Illusion

Not every device gets every feature. The split is sharper than Apple’s marketing suggests.

iPhone 11 through iPhone 14 will install iOS 27 and receive improvements like redesigned parental controls, Maps updates, Find My sharing options, and UI refinements. What they will not receive is anything requiring Apple Intelligence—Siri AI, Photos generative tools, Safari AI features, or context-aware suggestions in Mail and Messages. [Apple Headlines]

The most advanced on-device AI features—including expressive Siri voices and updated dictation—require the A19 Pro chipset, which means iPhone 17 Pro or later. [Tom’s Guide]

So there are effectively three tiers: old hardware gets performance fixes and UI polish; iPhone 15 and 16 get Apple Intelligence and Siri AI; iPhone 17 Pro and later get the full picture including the premium voice features. Apple hasn’t drawn that chart explicitly. But that’s what the announcements amount to.

The Release Schedule

iOS 27 was announced June 8, 2026. The developer beta is available now. A public beta arrives in July 2026. The full stable release ships in September 2026, alongside iPhone 18. [Apple Headlines]

iCloud Plus subscribers get expanded Apple Intelligence access, and some AI features—including image generation—carry daily usage limits that an active iCloud Plus subscription raises. [Memeburn]

One absence worth noting: there is at least one important feature still missing from iOS 27 [tomsguide] that reviewers flagged immediately after the keynote—though Apple has not specified a timeline for it. More details should emerge as the beta progresses through July.

The update ships in September. The beta is open now. The only thing left is to decide how much of the AI stack matters to you—and whether your hardware is in the right tier to use it.
