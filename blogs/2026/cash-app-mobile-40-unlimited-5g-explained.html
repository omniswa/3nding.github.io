Cash App Mobile: $40 Unlimited 5G Explained

June 12, 2026 7-Minute Read

Your phone bill and your bank account have always been separate problems. Cash App just decided that’s no longer true.

On June 11, 2026, Cash App announced the pilot launch of Cash App Mobile — an unlimited 5G phone plan running on AT&T’s network, priced at $40 per month including all taxes and fees. [News-Press NOW] That last part matters. Most carriers advertise a base rate and then tack on $10 to $15 in fees. Cash App Mobile is designed specifically for what the company calls “Modern Earners” — young adults, underbanked consumers, and gig workers who depend on connectivity to manage daily life. [Cash App]

What You Actually Get for $40

The plan includes unlimited 5G data, talk and text, unlimited HD streaming, and 10 GB of monthly hotspot within the United States, plus data roaming in Canada and Mexico. [Cash App] One plan. No tiers, no upsells.

The service is currently rolling out as a pilot for select Cash App users, with wider availability planned in the coming months. [Digital Trends] If you don’t have access yet, you’re waiting on a broader rollout — not a product announcement that never materializes.

Activation is handled entirely through an eSIM, which means no SIM card shipped to your address, no store visit, no waiting. You sign up inside the Cash App, and your phone connects. That frictionless setup is intentional: the population Cash App is targeting is exactly the group most likely to abandon a sign-up process the moment it gets inconvenient.

The Company Behind the Plan Isn’t Cash App

This is the part most headlines gloss over. The plan is powered by Gigs, a company that helps brands launch embedded mobile services. [Digital Trends] Gigs is an MVNO infrastructure provider — meaning it handles all the backend logistics of running a wireless service so that brands like Cash App don’t have to build carrier operations from scratch.

Cash App Mobile uses the same infrastructure provider as Klarna’s mobile service, which launched in June 2025 at identical $40 pricing. [KuCoin] Sezzle followed with its own version in February 2026. This is a pattern, not a coincidence. Gigs is essentially selling a white-label phone carrier in a box, and fintech companies are buying it because it locks users deeper into their ecosystems.

The underlying network is AT&T’s. That means the actual signal quality you experience is AT&T signal quality — not something Cash App controls, not something Gigs controls. If AT&T’s coverage is strong where you live, Cash App Mobile will work well. If it isn’t, no amount of app design fixes that.

Why a Payments App Is Selling Phone Plans

According to a 2026 Cash App survey, 46% of U.S. respondents reported experiencing friction when paying recurring phone bills. [cash] Friction, in this context, means things like: the payment failed, the bill was confusing, the autopay didn’t work, or the money wasn’t there at the right time. These are not trivial complaints for someone living paycheck to paycheck.

Cash App’s answer isn’t to make the bill-paying experience smoother. It’s to eliminate the external bill entirely by becoming the carrier. When Cash App is both the place you keep money and the company you owe money to, the transaction becomes frictionless by design. There’s no third party to fail.

That’s the logic. And it’s the same logic every super app has used to justify expansion — from WeChat in China to Grab in Southeast Asia. You don’t need users to love your new feature. You just need them to stop leaving the app.

The Ecosystem Play That Makes This More Than a Phone Plan

Cash App has confirmed plans to connect the mobile service to its Cash App Green rewards program and Cash App Families, its feature set for managing supervised child and teen accounts. [KuCoin] The Green rewards program already connects everyday spending to benefits inside the app.

Connecting a monthly wireless bill to that same rewards infrastructure could mean users earn Bitcoin rewards simply for paying their phone bill. [KuCoin] Block has been integrating Bitcoin into Cash App since 2018 — trading, Lightning Network support, crypto-linked rewards. A $40 recurring monthly payment is exactly the kind of high-frequency touchpoint that makes a loyalty program feel valuable.

This is why the $40 price point is almost beside the point. The plan could cost $45 and still be worth it to Cash App if it means users are running their finances, their investments, and their phone bill all in the same app. Churn drops. Engagement rises. The unit economics of acquiring a new user get much better when that user has three or four financial relationships with you instead of one.

How It Compares to What’s Already Out There

The $40 price point undercuts the big three carriers’ unlimited plans by a meaningful margin while matching what Klarna charges for its identical Gigs-powered service. [KuCoin]

For context: AT&T’s own unlimited starter plan runs around $65 a month before taxes and fees. Verizon and T-Mobile are in similar territory. MVNOs — carriers that lease network capacity rather than own towers — have always competed on price. Mint Mobile, Visible, and Cricket have built entire businesses around the gap between what it costs to use a network and what the major carriers charge retail customers.

Cash App Mobile is an MVNO. The difference is that most MVNOs are trying to sell you a phone plan. Cash App is trying to sell you a financial life, and the phone plan is part of the bundle.

Who Should Actually Sign Up

The honest answer: people who are already heavy Cash App users and live in areas with strong AT&T coverage.

There are no long-term contracts, no credit checks, and no store visits required. [cash] That combination is genuinely useful for people who have been turned away by traditional carriers due to credit history, or who don’t want to be locked into a two-year agreement. It’s also useful for gig workers whose income is variable and who need a plan they can manage month-to-month without penalty.

If you’re currently paying $65 or more for an unlimited plan on a major carrier and you’re an AT&T coverage area, switching saves you $25 to $35 a month. That’s $300 to $420 a year. It doesn’t sound dramatic until you calculate it.

What you’re giving up is potentially customer service infrastructure — major carriers have physical retail locations for device issues. Cash App Mobile is a digital-first, app-managed service. If something breaks, you’re solving it through the app or on the phone, not in a store.

The Bigger Shift This Represents

Klarna did it in 2025. Sezzle followed in early 2026. Cash App launched June 11, 2026. Three major fintech platforms have now entered the wireless business inside of twelve months, all using the same backend provider, all priced identically, all targeting the same underserved demographic.

This is not a trend. This is an infrastructure play becoming a product category. Gigs has quietly built the plumbing that makes it possible for any consumer brand with a large enough user base to become a phone carrier in weeks — and the fintech sector, which already has the financial relationship and the app, is the obvious first mover.

The traditional carrier model assumes you come to them for connectivity and only connectivity. Cash App’s Head of Business Owen Jennings framed it plainly: “Modern Earners deserve mobile service that works as hard as they do.” [cash] That’s marketing language, but it describes a real structural bet — that the next competitive frontier in wireless isn’t network quality or 5G speed. It’s financial integration.

Phone plans are recurring bills. Recurring bills are financial products. And Cash App has been in the financial products business for years. They’re not entering a new industry. They’re extending into an adjacent one with the same tools they already have.

That’s either very smart or very overextended. The next twelve months will tell us which.
