ID@Xbox Indie Selects Demo Fest: June 2026

June 5, 2026 8-Minute Read

Most demo events vanish before you realize they exist. This one runs the entire month of June.

The ID@Xbox Indie Selects Demo Fest went live on June 4, 2026, with more than 30 standout game demos available to play across Xbox Series X|S, Xbox One, and Xbox on PC. All demos are free, and you have until June 30 to work through them. [XBOX Wire] That’s a full four weeks—no scrambling, no missing the window because you were busy on a Tuesday.

What ID@Xbox Actually Is, and Why This Event Exists

ID@Xbox is Microsoft’s independent developer program. It gives indie studios access to Xbox development kits, publishing support, and visibility they couldn’t get otherwise. The Demo Fest is the public-facing payoff of that program: a concentrated moment where players can directly test unreleased games and send feedback back to the developers.

That feedback loop is the point. These aren’t polished vertical slices built for a trade show floor.

As Microsoft notes, these are early looks at games still in development, so the final versions may differ at launch — including availability by country as developers continue polishing their work. [XBOX Wire] Some of these games will change significantly before they ship. What you’re playing is a working conversation, not a final product.

That distinction matters. If you encounter something rough, report it. The developers are watching.

Five Demos Worth Your Time First

Forty-one demos in one month sounds exciting until you’re staring at a menu trying to decide where to start. Genre taste is personal, but based on what’s been announced, these five stand out for distinct reasons:

Order Automatica is the one to watch for tactics fans. Built on a simple 3×3 grid, it rewards smart positioning, timing, and synergy — inspired by Balatro, Inscryption, and Into the Breach. Simple rules, endless strategies. [xbox] That’s a very specific design lineage, and each of those three games delivered on exactly that promise.

Canvas of Thoughts is unusual. It’s a narrative experience where emotion shapes every conversation — stepping into the world of Evi, an 11-year-old girl navigating school and sensory input that can feel overwhelming, unpredictable, or quietly comforting. Rather than explain autism, it invites players to experience one perspective from within. [xbox] That framing alone is rare enough to be worth your hour.

Faeland is the sprawling one. A 2D action-RPG with a single connected map that rewards curiosity with shortcuts, secrets, and clever challenges — with combat emphasizing timing and spacing across swords, axes, spears, whips, shields, and bows, plus a substantial underwater region with its own movement and enemies. [xbox] If that description sounds like a lot, it is. That’s the point.

Surfpunk covers the co-op slot. A 1–4 player co-op Surf ‘n’ Slash adventure where surfer-pirates ride jet-powered boards across procedurally generated islands, battling monster swarms and chasing loot in a vibrant, anime-inspired tropical setting. [xbox] It sounds ridiculous. That’s not a criticism.

Grim Trials earns attention for its structure. You play as Avelin, a young woman recruited as one of Death’s Reapers, surviving the underworld’s trials with customizable gear and a flexible skill tree that shapes every run — along with unlockable blessings that support your playstyle. [xbox] A heavy-metal action-roguelite with a protagonist who’s already dead at the start has a better hook than most games that take 40 hours to get interesting.

How to Actually Find Everything

This is the part that trips people up every time.

All demos are featured in the dedicated Indie Selects Demo Fest collection on the Xbox Store. [XBOX Wire] Search for that collection directly rather than hunting title by title — trying to find 41 demos through individual store searches is a losing strategy.

On PC, access it through the Xbox app or Xbox.com. The collection will surface everything in one place. Individual store page links for each title are also live through the Xbox Wire announcement.

Regional availability varies. Some demos may not be accessible in certain countries at launch, but that can change as developers update their regional settings throughout the month. If something doesn’t appear for you immediately, it’s worth checking back.

Why This Format Works Better Than a Steam Next Fest Comparison Suggests

The reflex comparison is Steam Next Fest, and it’s a fair one — but there’s a structural difference that changes how you should approach this.

Steam Next Fest runs for about a week. This runs a full month. The longer window is deliberate: it’s designed to generate sustained feedback rather than a spike of day-one impressions. A developer gets more useful data from 10,000 players across three weeks than from 30,000 players on one Thursday.

The feedback piece is the one most players skip. Don’t. Microsoft specifically encourages sharing thoughts with developers via the ID@Xbox social channels on Twitter, Instagram, Discord, Twitch, Bluesky, and YouTube. [XBOX Wire] These are small studios. A direct message from a real player explaining what felt off about a control scheme can end up in a patch note.

That’s not a hypothetical. It’s how the program is designed to work. The demos exist to test games, not just market them. The distinction is real, and it’s the reason the better indie titles on Xbox have improved between demo and launch for years.

Dates, Platforms, Availability

  • Dates: June 4–30, 2026
  • Platforms: Xbox Series X|S, Xbox One, Xbox on PC
  • Cost: Free
  • Where to find it: Indie Selects Demo Fest collection on the Xbox Store or Xbox.com
  • Demos after June 30: Most will be removed from the store when the event ends

That last point is worth repeating. These demos are not permanent. The window closes at the end of the month, and the collection disappears with it. Thirty titles tried across four weekends is achievable. Forty-one is a stretch, but so is any list worth attempting.

Start with what genuinely interests you. Go from there.
