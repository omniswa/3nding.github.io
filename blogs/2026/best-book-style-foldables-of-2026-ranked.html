Best Book-Style Foldables of 2026: Ranked

May 16, 2026 12-Minute Read

The foldable phone market spent years being a Samsung monologue. Then Google showed up. Now Motorola has walked in with a 6,000mAh battery, three 50MP cameras, and a $1,899 price tag, and the conversation has genuinely changed. If you’re trying to decide between the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7, the Google Pixel 10 Pro Fold, and the Motorola Razr Fold in 2026, you’re choosing between three very different visions of what a big-screen foldable should be — and none of them is obviously wrong.

What You’re Actually Comparing

These three phones share a form factor and a general price band, but they diverge sharply on almost every meaningful axis.

The Galaxy Z Fold 7 launched in July 2025 at $1,999. It’s the thinnest and lightest book-style foldable Samsung has ever made — 4.2mm unfolded, 215 grams — powered by a Snapdragon 8 Elite for Galaxy and a 200MP main camera. Samsung has six generations of institutional knowledge baked into its hinge, its software, and its ecosystem.

The Pixel 10 Pro Fold arrived in October 2025 at $1,799. Google’s third-generation foldable added IP68 dust and water resistance, a gearless hinge, and a Tensor G5 chip built on TSMC’s 3nm N3E process. Its main selling point has never been the hardware spec sheet — it’s the software, the on-device AI, and seven years of guaranteed OS updates.

The Motorola Razr Fold is the new arrival, hitting shelves on May 21, 2026, for $1,899. This is Motorola’s first book-style foldable after years of dominating the clamshell space with its Razr flip line. It’s packing a 6,000mAh silicon-carbon battery, a triple-camera system with three 50MP sensors, and optional Moto Pen Ultra stylus support. First impressions from reviewers suggest Motorola got more right than wrong for a debut.

Design and the Feel of Carrying It

This is where the Z Fold 7 separates itself most clearly, and it matters more than most spec comparisons acknowledge.

At 215 grams and 4.2mm unfolded, the Fold 7 is lighter than the Galaxy S25 Ultra. For a phone with an 8-inch inner display, that’s genuinely remarkable. After carrying one for a few months, going back to the Pixel 10 Pro Fold — which weighs in at 258 grams and measures 5.2mm unfolded — feels like picking up a brick. The Pixel is heavier than the original iPad mini. That’s not hyperbole; it’s a real friction point if you’re handling the phone all day.

The Razr Fold lands between them at 243 grams and 4.7mm. Thicker and heavier than the Fold 7, but not as egregious as the Pixel. Engadget’s reviewer noted the extra heft is tolerable after a week, which is roughly the bar you’d hope a $1,900 device clears.

Samsung’s Armor FlexHinge upgrade this generation meaningfully reduces visible creasing and uses a multi-rail structure that distributes stress more evenly across the fold. Motorola went with a stainless steel hinge and a titanium plate behind the display to minimize crease — a reasonable approach from a first-timer. The Pixel’s gearless hinge is elegant in a different way: fewer mechanical parts theoretically means fewer things that can break, and Google’s IP68 certification is something neither Samsung nor Motorola matches on their inner display. The Fold 7 and Pixel both have IP48 on the inner display, but only the Pixel carries the full IP68 rating including full dust protection.

Cover display design tells you a lot about each company’s priorities. Samsung widened the Fold 7’s outer screen to a 21:9 aspect ratio, which makes it feel like a regular smartphone when closed — no more squinting at a narrow portrait window. The Pixel’s 6.4-inch outer display is good but not as usable. Motorola’s 6.6-inch pOLED cover screen is the largest of the three, and it works well for standalone use, though Motorola’s history with app optimization on cover displays trails Samsung’s.

Performance: Does the Chip Difference Actually Matter?

The Galaxy Z Fold 7 and the Razr Fold tell an interesting story here. Both use Qualcomm silicon, but not the same chip. Samsung has the Snapdragon 8 Elite for Galaxy — a binned, higher-clocked version — while Motorola uses the Snapdragon 8 Gen 5, which is a distinct chip with lower clock speeds and a less capable GPU and modem. In sustained, heavy tasks, the gap is real, even if most users won’t hit it in day-to-day use.

Google’s Tensor G5 is a different kind of chip. On pure benchmark numbers it trails both Qualcomm processors, but Tensor chips are designed to accelerate specific AI workloads on-device rather than win Geekbench. Real-world performance on the Pixel 10 Pro Fold is smooth for productivity and multitasking — the chip design is deliberately shaped around the software.

For most people’s actual usage — juggling a few split-screen apps, doing video calls, taking photos — none of these phones will feel slow. Where the Snapdragon 8 Elite pulls ahead is sustained GPU-heavy work: editing long 4K clips on the inner display, extended gaming sessions, that sort of thing. If that’s your use case, the Fold 7 is the clear choice. If you spend more of your time in documents, email, and AI-assisted writing, the Pixel’s Tensor G5 is optimized for exactly that and doesn’t feel like a compromise.

Cameras: Three Different Philosophies

This is the most nuanced category in this comparison, and the answer isn’t as obvious as the megapixel math suggests.

The Galaxy Z Fold 7 has a 200MP main camera — a first for a Samsung foldable — alongside a 12MP ultrawide and a 10MP 3x telephoto. Samsung’s ProVisual Engine processes images quickly, and the 4x-more-detail claim over the Z Fold 6 holds up in daylight. The 10x and 30x zoom shots are sharp enough for serious photography. Samsung’s processing philosophy tends toward punchy, vibrant results. In practice, that reads well on social media but occasionally oversaturates.

The Pixel 10 Pro Fold uses a 48MP main sensor, a 10.5MP ultrawide, and a 10.8MP 5x telephoto. The telephoto with 5x optical reach is a meaningful advantage over the Z Fold 7’s 3x for anything shot at a distance. But the 10MP ultrawide is genuinely underwhelming at this price. Google’s Night Sight is still the best night photography mode in the business, and the software post-processing on the Pixel consistently extracts more usable detail from difficult lighting than competing systems. It doesn’t win on raw resolution or versatility — it wins on consistency and trustworthiness in tough conditions.

The Motorola Razr Fold comes with three 50MP cameras: a main Sony LYT-828 sensor at f/1.6 with OIS, a 50MP ultrawide that doubles as a macro, and a 50MP 3x telephoto with 100x digital zoom. DXOMARK gave it a Gold Rating, calling it the “#1 foldable camera system.” In real-world comparisons, reviewers from Engadget found Razr Fold photos were consistently sharper and more detailed than the Z Fold 7 in both bright and low light, though Google’s Night Sight still edges it in very dark conditions. The one caveat: Motorola’s processing has a tendency to boost exposure, which causes color bleeding in vibrant subjects — the azalea-bush problem that Tom’s Guide documented is a real quirk.

The non-obvious insight here: the Pixel 10 Pro Fold’s camera system is increasingly hard to justify for the money. Its 10MP ultrawide and telephoto lenses feel like holdovers from a previous era in a year when Motorola shipped triple 50MP sensors on a foldable debut. Pixel shooters on the standard Pixel 10 Pro and 10 Pro XL are excellent; the foldable version made compromises that show.

Battery Life: Motorola’s Biggest Win

This isn’t close, and it matters.

The Razr Fold’s 6,000mAh silicon-carbon battery lasted 24 hours and 22 minutes in Engadget’s local video rundown test on the inner display. The Z Fold 7 hit just under 21 hours. The Pixel 10 Pro Fold reached 21 hours 20 minutes. The Razr Fold’s silicon-carbon cells are more energy-dense than standard lithium-ion, less prone to swelling, and support faster charging speeds as a byproduct of the chemistry.

On charging: the Razr Fold does 80W wired and 50W wireless. That’s a significant jump over the Z Fold 7 (25W wired, 15W wireless) and especially the Pixel 10 Pro Fold (30W wired, 15W wireless). The catch is Motorola uses a proprietary TurboPower standard for those speeds — you’ll need a Motorola adapter rather than a standard USB-PD or Qi2 charger to hit peak performance. Neither phone includes a charger in the box, so budget accordingly.

The Pixel’s charging situation — 30W wired in 2025, on a phone that costs $1,799 — is the most legitimate complaint in this comparison. Google has been slow here for years, and the gap between 30W and 80W is felt every morning.

Software and the Ecosystem Factor

Here’s the comparison most spec sheets don’t capture well.

Samsung’s One UI 8 on the Z Fold 7 has been refined over six generations of foldable hardware. TaskBar, Flex Mode, multi-window gestures, and second-screen continuity work the way you’d expect because Samsung has had years to identify and fix the edge cases. The integration with Galaxy AI, Galaxy Watch, Samsung DeX, and other Samsung products is deep. If you’re already in that ecosystem, the friction cost of leaving is real.

Google’s Android 16 on the Pixel 10 Pro Fold benefits from first-party software updates that arrive faster than any other Android device. The Pixel’s AI features — particularly the on-device summarization and call screening — run on Tensor hardware that’s purpose-built for those tasks. Seven years of OS updates is now table stakes for premium foldables (Samsung, Google, and Motorola all offer it), but the Pixel’s track record of delivering meaningful software improvements year over year is better than Motorola’s.

Motorola’s software is clean — closer to stock Android than Samsung’s One UI — with foldable-specific additions like a desk display mode in tent position, laptop mode that treats the hinge as a keyboard-screen split, and Catch Me Up (a notification summarizer). The Moto Pen Ultra stylus, sold separately for $99, works on both the inner and outer displays, which is more than Samsung offered on the Z Fold 7 (the Fold 7 dropped S Pen support entirely in favor of the thinner design). GrapheneOS support has also been confirmed for the Razr Fold, which matters to a specific but serious segment of privacy-focused buyers.

The question of software support longevity is worth flagging: Motorola’s historical track record on updates was spotty before this generation, and promising seven years is meaningfully different from delivering seven years. Samsung and Google have the receipts. Motorola is asking you to trust a new commitment.

Stylus: Samsung Abandoned It, Motorola Picked It Up

The S Pen’s absence on the Z Fold 7 is a calculated trade-off. Samsung removed it specifically to achieve the thinner, lighter design — and the result speaks for itself at 4.2mm and 215 grams. Whether that trade-off works for you depends entirely on whether you used the S Pen in the first place. Most people didn’t, and those who did can still use it with older Fold hardware.

The Moto Pen Ultra at $99 is sold separately and requires you to carry a charging case for the stylus separately from the phone, since there’s no storage slot. Reviewers have been honest: most of the time they left the stylus at home. Functionally, it’s better than nothing and appreciably better than Samsung’s current situation, but it’s not as integrated as the S Pen was when it lived inside the device.

Pixel has no stylus story whatsoever.

Price, Value, and the Honest Verdict

Phone Price (base) Weight Battery Charging Years of Updates
Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7 $1,999 215g 4,400 mAh 25W / 15W 7
Google Pixel 10 Pro Fold $1,799 258g 5,015 mAh 30W / 15W 7
Motorola Razr Fold $1,899 243g 6,000 mAh 80W / 50W 7

Buy the Galaxy Z Fold 7 if design and feel matter most to you, you’re already in the Samsung ecosystem, or you want the most refined, proven foldable experience available. It’s the most expensive of the three, but it’s also the most polished. The 200MP camera and Snapdragon 8 Elite are serious hardware, and no other phone in this category comes close on form factor.

Buy the Pixel 10 Pro Fold if you prioritize software longevity, on-device AI, and consistent camera performance over raw specs — and if you can forgive the weight. The lower price helps, especially now that the Pixel 10 Pro Fold is routinely discounted several months post-launch. It’s also the most durable of the three by IP rating, which is a non-trivial consideration for a $1,799 device with a fold line down the middle.

Buy the Motorola Razr Fold if battery life and charging speed are your primary pain points with existing foldables, you want a capable camera system that competes with the best, and you’re willing to be an early adopter on Motorola’s new form factor. The 6,000mAh silicon-carbon cell is a genuine differentiator, the cameras outperform expectations for a first-generation product, and the price sits between its competitors. The open question is whether Motorola’s software support promise is something you’re willing to bet three or four years on.

One thing worth sitting with: the Motorola Razr Fold is better hardware than its first-generation status suggests, but the Z Fold 7 and Pixel 10 Pro Fold have a year of real-world durability data behind them. A foldable that holds up is worth more than specs on a comparison table, and that data simply doesn’t exist for Motorola’s book-style debut yet.

The foldable market has gotten genuinely competitive. That’s a good problem for buyers to have.
