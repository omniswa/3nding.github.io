Rayman Legends Retold: October 1 Launch Guide

June 3, 2026 6-Minute Read

Ubisoft couldn’t keep this one quiet for more than a week. Leaks started circulating in late May 2026, the logo surfaced early, the price dropped on Dealabs before any official word—and then Sony’s State of Play on June 2 made it all official anyway. Ubisoft announced Rayman Legends Retold during Sony’s State of Play, confirming an October 1 launch on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2, GeForce Now, Blacknut, and PC via Ubisoft Connect, Steam, and the Epic Games Store. [Yahoo Finance]

So: it’s real, it’s October 1, and it’s $39.99.

This Is a Reimagining, Not a Remaster—That Distinction Matters

The word “remaster” gets thrown around loosely, usually meaning higher resolution and a stable frame rate. This is something bigger. Rayman Legends Retold features stunning 3D visuals that break up the 2D perception by bending the playground, tilting the camera, and dynamizing in-game cutscenes. [PlayStation] That’s a meaningful structural change to how the game is experienced, not just how it looks.

The original Rayman Legends was a 2D side-scroller. A flat plane. Gorgeous, but flat. Retold keeps the platforming logic intact while introducing depth and camera movement—which changes spatial reading, pacing, and the feel of traversal. Players who’ve played the original will notice this immediately.

For the first time in franchise history, the characters are fully voiced, giving them a new dimension. [PlayStation] That’s not a trivial addition. Voice acting defines personality in ways visual design alone can’t. It changes how players emotionally connect with a cast that’s historically been expressive but silent.

What’s Actually New vs. What’s Carried Over

This is the question that matters most for anyone who’s already put time into the 2013 original. Here’s the breakdown based on official announcements:

Feature Original (2013) Retold (2026)
Visual style 2D side-scroller 3D/2.5D with dynamic camera
Story Minimal Brand-new with fully voiced cinematics
Co-op Local only, up to 4 Local up to 4 (online TBC)
Musical levels 4 iconic stages 4 new stages added
Realms 5 worlds 5 + 1 brand-new sixth realm
Characters voiced No Yes, fully voiced
Soundtrack Christophe Héral Héral plus Grant Kirkhope [PlayStation]

New gameplay sequences include dragon-riding sections, four newly created music levels, and challenges within the Cave of Trials. [Noisy Pixel] The Cave of Trials functions as ongoing free challenges—meaning post-launch content without requiring a separate purchase. That’s meaningful for longevity.

The Studios Behind It and Why It Matters

Development credits signal a lot about a game’s DNA. Rayman Legends Retold is led by Ubisoft Montpellier and Ubisoft Milan. [Yahoo Finance] Montpellier is the studio that made the original—Michel Ancel’s team, the people who invented this version of Rayman. Their involvement isn’t ceremonial. It means institutional memory of what made the original work is present in the room.

Ubisoft Milan’s co-credit is notable. They’ve handled ports and supporting development work across Ubisoft’s catalog. A joint lead credit suggests this is a substantive collaborative effort, not one studio doing the heavy lifting while another stamps their name on it.

The Soundtrack Addition Is Bigger Than It Sounds

Grant Kirkhope joining Christophe Héral on the expanded soundtrack deserves its own sentence. Kirkhope composed Banjo-Kazooie, GoldenEye, Donkey Kong 64, and Mario + Rabbids. He understands comedy, wonder, and kinetic energy in music the same way Héral does. Putting them together on a platformer that has always used music as a core gameplay mechanic—particularly in the musical levels—is a combination that should produce something special.

The four new musical stages require every move to sync with the beat of the soundtrack. [PlayStation] This is the mechanical reason the soundtrack matters: the music isn’t background texture. It’s the level design. A weak or mismatched score makes these stages feel arbitrary. A strong one makes them feel inevitable.

Editions, Pricing, and What Pre-Orders Get You

The standard edition is priced at $39.99 and pre-orders for any edition receive the Hoodlum Havoc Pack—two costumes for Rayman and Globox inspired by Rayman 3: Hoodlum Havoc. [Yahoo Finance]

The Standard Edition includes the base game and Rayman Origins: Enhanced Edition—the original 2D adventure now running at 4K, 60 FPS, with haptic feedback support and quality-of-life improvements. [PlayStation]

The Deluxe Edition adds the Retro Pack with four costumes for Rayman, Globox, Barbara, and Grand Minimus, plus an in-game Art Gallery. [PlayStation]

The $39.99 price point is the right call. At $60 or $70, it would face a credibility problem against full-price new releases. At $40, it sits cleanly in the “obvious yes” category for anyone who liked the original—and positions itself as an accessible entry point for players who never played it.

Bundling Rayman Origins: Enhanced Edition into the standard price is genuinely generous. Origins is a full game, and its own enhancements—4K, 60 FPS, haptic feedback—are substantive. It functions as both a bonus and a backdoor into the franchise’s wider history.

Physical Edition: Not Universal

A detail worth knowing before you search retailer shelves on October 1. A physical Game-Key Card release has been confirmed, but it is limited to PS5 and Nintendo Switch 2. [My Nintendo News] Xbox Series X|S players and PC players will be digital-only. This matters if you buy physical as a preference or for collection purposes—plan accordingly.

Where This Sits in the Broader Rayman Picture

Rayman Legends came out in 2013 and was widely considered one of the best platformers of that generation. Then Rayman went quiet. A proposed Rayman Adventures and various mobile iterations didn’t sustain the franchise’s momentum. Michel Ancel left Ubisoft in 2020.

A reimagining of the franchise’s creative high point—with new story, new voice work, new music, and a new realm—is the most substantial investment Ubisoft has made in Rayman since then. Whether it re-establishes the franchise or functions as a one-time nostalgia release will depend on reception. But the scope of changes suggests Ubisoft is treating this as more than a quick revenue move.

Brand Producer Loïc Gounon described the project as wanting to preserve everything fans love while using Legends as a foundation to expand Rayman’s lore, world logic, and how everything connects together. [PlayStation] That’s a producer talking about architecture, not surface. It suggests they’ve thought about what this fictional world means structurally—not just visually.

October 1 is a Wednesday. Pre-orders are live now across all platforms.
